## Aim

After the introduction the reader can state the limitation of federated
fine-tuning for Text-to-SQL that the paper addresses, the observation
that makes it addressable, what \method does about it and by how much
it improves over FedAvg SFT; and can locate the novelty against FedAvg
SFT and against FedDPO. After the abstract alone the reader retains the
same four elements and the headline number.

## Points of the argument

1. Context: Text-to-SQL for natural-language access to relational
   databases; highest-accuracy systems rely on prompting proprietary
   large language models, transmitting questions and database schemas to
   external providers; small language models (SLMs; 0.5B to 1.5B
   parameters) permit local fine-tuning and on-premise deployment.
   Evidence: the cited systems.
2. Setting: individual organizations possess limited databases and
   annotated questions, while data isolation precludes centralizing
   multi-organization corpora; collaborative training of a shared SLM via
   federated learning, predominantly FedAvg SFT of a LoRA adapter.
   Scope definition (stated once): "federated Text-to-SQL" denotes
   federated training of the Text-to-SQL model. Evidence: cited federated
   fine-tuning studies; the prior study on federated semantic parsing.
3. Limitations of existing federated training, two, with their shared
   structural cause:
   (a) FedAvg SFT: supervision restricted to one gold SQL query per
   question; client databases enter solely as prompt text, without
   execution of either the gold SQL query or model-generated queries and
   without execution feedback in the training objective; absence of
   supervision on incorrect queries generated by the model.
   (b) FedDPO: federated preference optimization over static preference
   datasets whose paired responses are external to the model under
   training, against a single fixed reference model across all
   communication rounds; inability to target the evolving errors of the
   trained model.
   Shared cause: query verification requires execution against the
   underlying relational database, which resides exclusively at the
   client.
   Evidence: formal definitions (Section 3.1; the cited paper).
4. Key insight: client-side query execution converts a single gold SQL
   query into multi-sample execution-derived supervision over the
   model's own generated queries (partitioned into correct and incorrect
   samples by execution-result equivalence with the gold SQL query on
   the client database). Fig. 1 illustrates both training paradigms on a
   single question; running text avoids repeating the figure's concrete
   example.
5. Proposed framework: \method, a federated learning framework for
   Text-to-SQL, comprising four components mapped to limitations (a) and
   (b): supervised warm-up; client-side execution labeling (addresses
   (a)); on-policy pair construction (addresses (b): preference pairs
   and reference model renewed at every communication round from the
   current global model, aligning one preference-optimization iteration
   with one communication round); preference objective (preference term
   plus token-averaged NLL term on the correct sample). Communication
   footprint per round identical to FedAvg SFT: LoRA adapter plus a
   single integer aggregation weight. Evidence: Section 3.
6. Empirical validation: at equal communication rounds, +6.19 EX over
   FedAvg SFT on BIRD dev and +3.49 EX on Spider test (both
   statistically significant); FedAvg SFT plateaus by round 3 on both
   benchmarks (+5.08 EX over the peak FedAvg SFT round on BIRD dev);
   approximately 50% reduction in execution errors among evaluation
   predictions on both benchmarks (statistically significant). Evidence:
   Table 3 and Section 5.1; Section 5.2.
7. Contributions: four parallel items (specified under Materials).

The abstract carries points 2 to 6 in the five functions of
`voice.md`: context (federated training of an SLM for Text-to-SQL),
limitation (3a, and 3b in a clause), framework by name with its
mechanism (execution of the model's own queries at the client;
preference pairs renewed each round), results (the BIRD dev gain first,
then Spider test, then the execution errors), implication (client
databases as supervision at unchanged communication).

Facts on these numbers:

- Significance: exact McNemar test on paired predictions, Holm
  correction within each table; one training seed.
- FedAvg SFT by round on BIRD dev: 42.70, 43.68, 42.57 at rounds 2, 3,
  4; on Spider test 74.71 at rounds 3 and 4, 73.92 at round 5.
- The difference in execution errors is tested per prediction and is
  significant on both datasets.
- The setup: five clients, non-IID partition by database, all clients
  in every round.

### Contributions

1. A source of supervision: client databases as supervision that
   federated fine-tuning does not exploit and that is accessible only at
   the client; to our knowledge the first federated training method
   for Text-to-SQL that uses it.
2. The framework \method: the four components; alignment of one
   preference-optimization iteration with one communication round; the
   round's global model as reference model; per-round client upload
   restricted to the adapter and its integer aggregation weight.
3. The evidence: the gain over FedAvg SFT on BIRD dev and Spider test
   at the same number of rounds; where it originates (execution
   errors; correct predictions over other tables or columns than those
   of the gold SQL query); the role of each component; the client
   cost.
4. The release: code, client splits and the predictions of every row
   of the tables.
