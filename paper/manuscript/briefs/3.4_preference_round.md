## Facts

### Preference round $t$, for $T_w < t \le T$

| Step | Actor | Operation | Values |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | server | sends $\theta^{t-1}$ to the clients | full participation: all $K$ clients in every round |
| 2 | client | sampling: for each $(q, d, y^\star) \in \mathcal{D}_k$, $N$ samples from $\pi_{\theta^{t-1}}(\cdot \mid x)$ | $N$ = `\setting{pref.samples}`; temperature `\setting{pref.t}`; no top-$p$ or top-$k$ truncation; at most `\setting{pref.tokens}` new tokens |
| 3 | client | labeling: executes $y^\star$ once and every sample on $d$. A sample $y$ is correct iff $\mathrm{exec}(y, d) = \mathrm{exec}(y^\star, d)$, with results compared as the execution accuracy of the benchmark compares them; otherwise incorrect | incorrect is one of two kinds: execution error (fails to execute, or exceeds the time limit) and incorrect result (executes, different result). An output that contains no SQL: incorrect. A sample truncated at the token limit: not labeled, enters no pair. The value of the time limit belongs to Appendix B and is not stated here |
| 4 | client | pair construction: for each question with at least one correct and one incorrect sample, $M$ pairs $(y^{+}, y^{-})$; $y^{+}$ uniform over its correct samples, $y^{-}$ uniform over its incorrect samples | $M$ = `\setting{pref.pairs}`. Drawn over the samples as generated: a query generated three times is three times as likely. Without replacement when the question has at least $M$ samples of that label, with replacement otherwise |
| 5 | client | local training: initialized at $\theta^{t-1}$; `\setting{pref.epochs}` epoch over $\mathcal{P}_k^{t}$ with the loss below; result $\theta_k^{t}$ | |
| 6 | client | uploads $\theta_k^{t}$ and $\lvert\mathcal{P}_k^{t}\rvert$ | adapter size: `\setting{adapter.mb}` |
| 7 | server | aggregation: $\theta^{t} = \sum_k w_k \theta_k^{t}$, $w_k = \lvert\mathcal{P}_k^{t}\rvert / \sum_j \lvert\mathcal{P}_j^{t}\rvert$, applied to every matrix of the adapter | in a warm-up round $w_k \propto n_k$ |

Questions that yield no pair in a round:

| Case | Reason |
|---|---|
| all samples correct, or all incorrect | no pair of opposite labels exists |
| the tables of the database contain no rows | the comparison of execution results is uninformative there |
| the gold SQL query fails to execute | no label can be assigned |

### Loss of one pair $(x, y^{+}, y^{-})$

Checked against the code.

$$ m_\theta = \beta \left[ \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y^{+} \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{+} \mid x)} - \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y^{-} \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{-} \mid x)} \right] $$

$$ \ell(\theta) = -\log \sigma(m_\theta) \; - \; \frac{\lambda}{|y^{+}|} \log \pi_\theta(y^{+} \mid x) $$

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| $\log \pi(y \mid x)$ | sum of the log-probabilities of the tokens of $y$ |
| $\lvert y^{+} \rvert$ | number of tokens of $y^{+}$ |
| $\sigma$ | logistic function |
| $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ | $\pi_{\theta^{t-1}}$; fixed during the round; its log-probabilities of the pairs are computed once, before local training |
| start of local training | $m_\theta = 0$ for every pair: model and reference model coincide |
| first term | the DPO loss (`rafailov2023dpo`) |
| second term | negative log-likelihood of the correct sample, averaged over its tokens |
| objective of client $k$ | mean of $\ell$ over $\mathcal{P}_k^{t}$ |
| $\beta$, $\lambda$ | `\setting{pref.beta}`, `\setting{pref.nll}` |

## References

| Key | Facts |
|---|---|
| `rafailov2023dpo` | DPO. A loss over pairs of a preferred and a dispreferred output that increases the likelihood of the preferred one relative to a fixed reference model; $\beta$ sets the strength of the constraint towards the reference model. The first term of the loss is the DPO loss |
| `tao2024codelutra` | CodeLutra, centralized, for code and SQL generation. Per iteration: 10 responses per input generated by the current model at temperature 1.0; labeled by execution against the gold result; correct paired with incorrect responses at random; trained with a DPO term whose reference model is the model of the current iteration, plus $\lambda$ times the log-likelihood of the correct response. $\beta$ = 0.1 and $\lambda$ = 1.0 for its data query task. Followed here: number of samples, temperature, random pairing, $\beta$, $\lambda$, and the reference model being the model that generated the samples. Different here: $M$ = 2 pairs per question where CodeLutra constructs 10; the NLL term is averaged over tokens where CodeLutra's is the log-likelihood of the sequence |
| `mcmahan2017fedavg` | FedAvg. Clients train locally from the global model; the server averages the client models, weighted by the amount of local data |

LoRA is introduced and cited in Section 3.1; it needs no citation here.
