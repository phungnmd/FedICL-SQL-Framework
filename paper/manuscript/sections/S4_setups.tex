\section{Federated setups}\label{sup:setups}

Each database, with all its questions, is assigned to one client, so that
a client is an organization that holds its databases. The difficulty of
a training question is its level under the criteria of the evaluation
script of Spider: easy, medium, hard or extra hard. For BIRD train, the
same criteria are applied to the query as parsed by sqlglot, since the
parser of the Spider script does not read many queries of BIRD.

In the IID setup, the databases are assigned at random in equal shares,
so the setup is IID over databases, not over questions. In a non-IID
setup, the databases are grouped into four tiers by the quartiles of
their share of hard and extra-hard questions. For each tier, the shares
of the clients are drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with
concentration $\alpha$, and the databases of the tier are assigned in
these shares. This is the label-distribution skew of NIID-Bench
(Section~\ref{sec:fed-setups} of the main paper cites it) with the tier
as label and the database as unit, including its rule that a client
that already holds its equal share of the databases when a tier is
assigned receives none of that tier. A client can thus exceed its equal
share within one tier. The partition is drawn again until every client
holds at least \fact{split.spider.a0.1.k5.min_dbs} databases
(\fact{split.spider.a0.5.k10.min_dbs} with
\fact{split.spider.a0.5.k10.clients} clients), from split seed
\setting{split.seed}. Table~\ref{tab:setups} gives the five setups.
