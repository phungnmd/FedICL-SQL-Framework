\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of the results}\label{sec:interpretation}

Two sets of tests bear on the two kinds of incorrect prediction
(Sections~\ref{sec:rq4} and~\ref{sec:ablation}). Against FedAvg SFT
after four rounds, \method{} has about half the execution errors, a
significant difference on both evaluation sets, whereas its larger
counts of incorrect results, \nwr{bird}{k2}{4} against
\nwr{bird}{fedavg}{4} on BIRD dev and \nwr{spider}{k2}{4} against
\nwr{spider}{fedavg}{4} on Spider test, are not a significant
difference. For the preference term in one round, significantly fewer
predictions end in an execution error with it than without and, on BIRD
dev, significantly more return an incorrect result. Part of the fall
against FedAvg SFT is a shift: on BIRD dev,
\fact{moved.bird.error.wrong} of its \nerr{bird}{fedavg}{4} execution
errors return an incorrect result with \method, of which no error warns
the user.

The gain in EX comes from both kinds: of the questions of BIRD dev that
only \method{} answers correctly, \fact{moved.bird.wrong.correct} had
an incorrect result with FedAvg SFT and \fact{moved.bird.error.correct}
an execution error. Nor does it rest on the preference term alone: a
round with the NLL term alone on the correct samples ends above the
model before it in EX on both sets (values of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), and a difference in EX to the round with
both terms is not established.

The pairs of the first preference round agree with the tested effect:
on BIRD, \fact{pairs.bird.pref1.neg_error} have a sample with an
execution error as their incorrect side and
\fact{pairs.bird.pref1.neg_wrong} one with an incorrect result
(\fact{pairs.spider.pref1.neg_error} and
\fact{pairs.spider.pref1.neg_wrong} on Spider), and the round lowers
the execution errors on both sets. The preference term is isolated in
this round only, and we do not attribute the changes of the second to
its pairs (\fact{pairs.bird.pref2.neg_error} and
\fact{pairs.bird.pref2.neg_wrong} on BIRD,
\fact{pairs.spider.pref2.neg_error} and
\fact{pairs.spider.pref2.neg_wrong} on Spider). On BIRD dev, the
execution errors rise from \nerr{bird}{k2}{3} to \nerr{bird}{k2}{4} and
the incorrect results fall from \nwr{bird}{k2}{3} to \nwr{bird}{k2}{4};
on Spider test, the execution errors fall further and the incorrect
results stay level.

For the warm-up rounds, the evaluation evidence is from the Spider sets
only. They leave fewer training questions without a correct sample at
the first preference round, their purpose in Section~\ref{sec:warmup},
yet EX on Spider test is level with and without them
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The tested difference is in TS
(Table~\ref{tab:robust}): the variant without warm-up rounds is below
\method{} on the three sets with test suites, significantly on
Spider-Syn only, and in values above FedAvg SFT in EX and below it in
TS. Two differences from \method{} could each lower its TS, and these
rows do not separate them: the variant never trains on a gold SQL
query, and it runs four preference rounds instead of two. On Spider-DK,
without a test suite, its EX is higher, though not significantly.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The study covers clients that hold gold SQL queries for their
questions, which excludes questions without one, and databases on which
the sampled queries are executed, read-only and under the time limit of
the evaluation; the load on a database in use is outside the study. The
five clients, ten in one setup, all take part in every round, are
simulated from the training set of one benchmark and differ in domain
and schema; the skew that is varied is query complexity
(Section~\ref{sec:fed-setups}). Partial participation, more than ten
clients and clients from different sources are not studied. \method{}
has \setting{rounds.pref} preference rounds among its
$T = \setting{rounds.total}$ rounds and is not trained further; only
the variant without warm-up rounds has more.

The data remain at the clients, which is data isolation and not
differential privacy: we make no claim about what an adapter reveals.
The number of pairs that a client sends as its weight depends on how
the model performs on its data, which its number of questions does not;
with the question counts as weights, EX after one preference round does
not differ significantly (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

An execution label compares execution results on one database: a
question on a database without rows yields no pair, and a query that is
not equivalent to the gold SQL query can return the same result. EX
compares on one database as well, and how much of the gain rests on
such queries is known only in part. On BIRD dev, the correct
predictions that read other tables or columns than the gold SQL query
rise from \fact{form.bird.fedavg.4.other_right} to
\fact{form.bird.k2.4.other_right} and those that read the same from
\fact{form.bird.fedavg.4.gold_right} to
\fact{form.bird.k2.4.gold_right}, whereas on Spider test the second
group has the larger rise (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}); the first count
does not say how many of its queries are not equivalent. For Spider,
TS, which can reject them~\cite{zhong2020testsuite}, keeps the
direction of the difference to FedAvg SFT on the three sets with test
suites (Section~\ref{sec:rq3}); BIRD has no test suite, and its gain
has no stricter check.

The experiments cover models of at most \setting{model.size} parameters
with LoRA, SQLite databases and questions in English. The number of
warm-up rounds is a setting, $T_w = \setting{rounds.warmup}$ for every
model and dataset outside the variants of Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.
Most rows rest on one training seed and one draw of the client
partition, and the test of Section~\ref{sec:stat-test} covers only the
variation over the evaluation questions. The additional cost of \method{}
is computation at the clients: a preference round takes between
\fact{cost.spider.pref2.ratio} and \fact{cost.bird.pref1.ratio} times
the client time of a FedAvg SFT round (Table~\ref{tab:cost}), and
communication is unchanged.
