\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets and metrics}\label{sec:datasets}

The clients hold the training set of BIRD~\cite{li2023bird},
\fact{split.bird.a0.1.k5.questions} questions on
\fact{split.bird.a0.1.k5.databases} databases, or that of
Spider~\cite{yu2018spider}, \fact{split.spider.a0.1.k5.questions}
questions on \fact{split.spider.a0.1.k5.databases} databases. Since the
test set of BIRD is not public, we evaluate on BIRD dev
(\fact{questions.bird} questions on \fact{suite.bird.databases}
databases) and on Spider test (\fact{questions.spider}). The models trained on Spider are also
evaluated on Spider dev (\fact{questions.spiderdev}) and on three sets
built from it: Spider-Syn~\cite{gan2021spidersyn} (\fact{questions.syn}),
with synonyms for schema words, Spider-Realistic~\cite{deng2021realistic}
(\fact{questions.realistic}), without explicit column names, and
Spider-DK~\cite{gan2021spiderdk} (\fact{questions.dk}), which requires
domain knowledge. Training and evaluation databases are disjoint.

Execution accuracy (EX) counts a prediction as correct when its
execution result equals that of the gold SQL query under the comparison
of the benchmark's evaluation, equality of the sets of rows on BIRD and
the result comparison of the test-suite evaluation script on Spider, and
under its time limit (Section~\ref{sup:labeling} of the Supplementary
Materials). An incorrect
prediction is an execution error when it fails to execute, reaches the
time limit or is cut at the token limit, and an incorrect result when it
executes and returns another result. Test-suite accuracy
(TS)~\cite{zhong2020testsuite} also requires the gold result on every
database of a suite of content variants. The official suite of the
Spider dev databases serves Spider dev, Spider-Syn and Spider-Realistic,
whose questions are posed on the same databases, and Spider test and
Spider-DK, which have none, are reported under EX alone.
For BIRD dev, we generate \fact{suite.variants} variants of each database
with the fuzzing procedure of the test-suite evaluation. Generated in the
same way for Spider dev, such a suite agrees with the official one on
\fact{suite.calib.agreement_ex_correct}\% of the
\fact{suite.calib.ex_correct} EX-correct predictions of our models, the
predictions on which the number of variants was chosen. No official suite
exists against which the suite of BIRD dev itself could be compared.

\subsection{Federated setups}\label{sec:fed-setups}

Each database, with all its questions, belongs to one client, so that a
client is an organization that holds its databases. The two main setups
assign the databases of BIRD train to \fact{split.bird.a0.1.k5.clients} clients and those
of Spider train to \fact{split.spider.a0.1.k5.clients}, non-IID in the
difficulty of the questions: the databases are grouped
into four tiers by their share of hard and extra-hard questions, and the
shares of the clients in each tier are drawn from a Dirichlet
distribution with $\alpha = {}$\fact{split.bird.a0.1.k5.alpha}, the
label-distribution skew of NIID-Bench~\cite{li2022niidbench}.
Section~\ref{sec:breadth} adds an independent and identically
distributed (IID) partition of Spider train and two with
$\alpha = {}$\fact{split.spider.a0.5.k5.alpha}, with
\fact{split.spider.a0.5.k5.clients} and
\fact{split.spider.a0.5.k10.clients} clients (Table~\ref{tab:setups},
Section~\ref{sup:setups} of the Supplementary Materials). All clients
take part in every round.

\subsection{Models and baselines}\label{sec:models}\label{sec:baselines}

All experiments use \setting{model}~\cite{hui2024qwen25coder}, except
those of Section~\ref{sec:breadth}, which add \fact{model.qwen05}-Instruct
and \fact{model.dsc13}-Instruct~\cite{guo2024deepseekcoder}, so every model
has \setting{models.size.min} to \setting{model.size} parameters. All
models share the adapter configuration, prompt and decoding. \textit{No
training} is the pretrained model. \textit{Local SFT} trains an adapter
at each client on its own data alone, and its row gives the mean, the
lowest and the highest EX over the clients. \textit{Centralized SFT}
trains one adapter on the union of the clients' data for
\setting{central.epochs} epochs. It pools the data that federated
training keeps apart and serves as a reference, not as a federated
baseline.
\textit{FedAvg SFT}~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg,ye2024openfedllm} runs
\setting{rounds.total} rounds, the first \setting{rounds.warmup} of which
are the warm-up rounds of \method, so that the two share that model
exactly. On Spider, it also runs a fifth round, which gives it more GPU
time than \method{} uses (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).
\textit{FedProx SFT}~\cite{li2020fedprox} adds a proximal term to its
local objective. \textit{FedDPO}~\cite{ye2024openfedllm} trains every
client in every round on one fixed preference dataset against one fixed
reference model with the DPO loss alone. As the clients hold no
preference data, we give it the execution-labeled pairs of the first
preference round of \method, from the same warm-up model, samples,
labels and $M$, and it keeps these pairs and this reference model in all
its rounds. Its other settings are those of the preference rounds, so its
first round is the first preference round of \method{} without the NLL
term.

\subsection{Implementation}\label{sec:implementation}

The adapter is LoRA of rank \setting{lora.r} with \texttt{lora\_alpha}
\setting{lora.alpha} on all attention and feed-forward projections,
from one initialization shared by all clients. Warm-up and FedAvg SFT
rounds train with \setting{sft.optimizer}~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, a
learning rate of \setting{sft.lr}, a learning-rate warm-up over
\setting{sft.warmup} and LoRA dropout \setting{sft.dropout}, and
preference rounds with \setting{pref.optimizer}, a learning rate of
\setting{pref.lr}, a learning-rate warm-up over \setting{pref.warmup}
and LoRA dropout \setting{pref.dropout}, so that the two kinds of round
differ in these four settings. An update takes \setting{sft.batch} examples in the
former and \setting{pref.batch} pairs in the latter, and the schedule,
like the optimizer, starts anew at every client in every round. The
number of samples per question, the sampling
temperature, $\beta$, the learning rate, the number of pairs per update,
the number of epochs and the weight decay of the preference rounds
follow CodeLutra~\cite{tao2024codelutra}, which constructs ten pairs per
question where we construct \setting{pref.pairs}. At evaluation, the
model decodes greedily with at most \setting{eval.tokens} new tokens and
no repetition penalty. Each model comes from one training run, with
seed \setting{seed} except in the rows of Table~\ref{tab:main} marked as
a second seed. The seed sets \setting{seed.role}. All runs use one GPU,
an \setting{hw.gpu}, and train the clients of a round one after another.
Tables~\ref{tab:settings} and~\ref{tab:settings-2} (Section~\ref{sup:settings} of the Supplementary Materials) list every
setting and the software.

\subsection{Statistical test}\label{sec:stat-test}

We compare two models on the same evaluation set with the exact McNemar
test~\cite{mcnemar1947note} on their paired verdicts per question, a
two-sided binomial test on the questions that exactly one of the two
models answers correctly, and adjust the $p$-values by the Holm correction~\cite{holm1979simple}
within each family: the tests of one table, a test printed twice
counting in the first, and the comparisons quoted only in the text. A difference is significant when its adjusted $p$ is below
\fact{test.alpha}. Every $p$ we print is adjusted, and the tables mark a
significant difference with an asterisk. The same test
compares the execution errors and the incorrect results of two models.
It covers the variation over evaluation questions, not between training
runs.
