\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Fine-tuned open models for Text-to-SQL}\label{sec:rel-slm}

Open language models fine-tuned for Text-to-SQL form an established line
of work, whose methods differ in the data and in the objective of
training. CodeS~\cite{li2024codes} pre-trains models of 1B to 15B
parameters further on a SQL-centric corpus and fine-tunes them on the
training set of each benchmark. OmniSQL~\cite{li2025omnisql} relies on
synthetic data, fine-tuning models of 7B to 32B parameters on
SynSQL-2.5M and the training sets of Spider and BIRD.
SPFT-SQL~\cite{zhang2025spftsql} synthesizes its data from the training
databases and fine-tunes by self-play. SLM-SQL~\cite{sheng2025slmsql},
Think2SQL~\cite{papicchio2025think2sql} and
Arctic-Text2SQL-R1~\cite{yao2026arctic} change the objective and train
the model by reinforcement learning with an execution-based reward, the
first of them for models of 0.5B to 1.5B parameters. All of these
methods assume training at one site, which holds the questions, the SQL
queries and the databases together.

\subsection{Preference optimization and execution feedback}\label{sec:rel-pref}

Execution can also supply a preference between two outputs instead of a
reward. DPO~\cite{rafailov2023dpo} trains a language model on pairs of a
preferred and a dispreferred output, against a reference model. For SQL,
the preference needs no annotation beyond the gold SQL query that SFT
already uses: sampled queries are executed, and the comparison with the
execution result of the gold SQL query decides which is preferred. The
same comparison also provides the labels for models that select among
candidate queries at inference, a verifier in LEVER~\cite{ni2023lever}
and an outcome reward model in GradeSQL~\cite{tritto2026gradesql},
without training the model that generates the queries.

Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025cotdpo} train ten Text-to-SQL models of 6.7B to
15B parameters with DPO on pairs labeled in this way. The pairs are
constructed once, from samples of the model after SFT, the loss is the
DPO loss alone, and that model is a fixed reference. They report that EX
on BIRD dev, with greedy decoding, rises after DPO for all ten models
when chain-of-thought reasoning precedes the query and falls for six
when the output is the query alone. Our model also outputs the query
alone, and Section~\ref{sec:ablation} varies all three of these
choices.

Other methods renew the pairs during training.
ExCoT~\cite{zhai2025excot}, also for Text-to-SQL, takes them first from
outputs of GPT-4o and then from outputs of the model in training.
Outside SQL, Pang et al.~\cite{pang2024irpo} iterate the whole
procedure: in each iteration, they sample answers from the current
model, pair an answer that is correct with one that is not, and train with a DPO and an NLL term, with the
model of the previous iteration as the reference.

The closest work to ours is CodeLutra~\cite{tao2024codelutra}, a method
for code generation evaluated on Text-to-SQL and on data-science code.
It applies this iterative scheme at one site, with execution labels:
each iteration pairs a correct with a failed sample of the current
model. \method{} takes from
CodeLutra its sampling settings, the scale of the DPO term, the uniform
pairing and the choice of reference model, and
Section~\ref{sec:pref-round} states where the two differ. In \method{},
the loop runs across clients: a federated round takes the place of the
iteration, and each client samples from the global model of the round,
which no single party trained, and labels the samples on its own
databases. Warm-up rounds of FedAvg SFT come first, fewer pairs are
constructed, and the NLL term is averaged over tokens.

\subsection{Federated fine-tuning of language models}\label{sec:rel-fed}

Language models are fine-tuned across clients with
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} and LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}: each client
trains an adapter on its own data with SFT, and the server averages the
adapters. FederatedScope-LLM~\cite{kuang2024fsllm} provides a package
and a benchmark for such fine-tuning, and
OpenFedLLM~\cite{ye2024openfedllm} a framework for federated instruction
tuning and value alignment.

DPO has also been used in federated settings, where the preferences are
data that the clients hold. In the FedDPO of
OpenFedLLM~\cite{ye2024openfedllm}, each client holds a preference
dataset, for helpfulness or harmlessness, so that the pairs are fixed
and are not outputs of the model being trained; the loss is the DPO loss
alone, and the reference model is fixed for the whole process. We run
FedDPO on Text-to-SQL as a baseline (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}). Wu et
al.~\cite{wu2025fedbiscuit} keep human preference data at the clients,
which train preference selectors, while the language model is trained
with DPO at the server.

A checkable answer has also supplied the signal in federated training:
PubSwap~\cite{nayak2026pubswap}, for mathematics and medical question
answering, and Fed-GRPO~\cite{guo2026fedgrpo}, for mathematics and code
generation, apply reinforcement learning at the clients with a reward
for a correct answer. None of the federated works of this subsection
considers Text-to-SQL.

\subsection{Federated semantic parsing and Text-to-SQL}\label{sec:rel-fedsql}

Federated learning for Text-to-SQL was studied earlier by Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2023fl4sp}, and a related work applies it to a semantic
parser for knowledge-graph questions~\cite{xu2024kgrfl}. Zhang et al.
take eight single-domain datasets as the clients and train a T5-base
model with supervision on the gold SQL. Their question is the
heterogeneity of these clients: FedAvg falls far below centralized
training, and Lorar, their re-weighting of the clients, recovers a large
part of the gap. The supervision is the gold SQL alone, and nothing is
executed, in training or in evaluation. Our question is what execution on the
databases of a client adds to the gold SQL as supervision.
