\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Federated SFT for Text-to-SQL learns from the gold SQL query of each
question and never executes a query on the databases that the clients
hold. \method{} uses these databases: after supervised warm-up rounds,
each client samples queries from the global model, labels them by
executing them against the gold result on its own databases, and trains
with a DPO term and an NLL term on preference pairs constructed anew in
every round, and the server averages the adapters.

At the same number of rounds, \method{} is above FedAvg SFT by
\gap{bird}{fedavg}{4}{k2}{4} EX points on BIRD dev and by
\gap{spider}{fedavg}{4}{k2}{4} on Spider test, with about half as many
execution errors while incorrect results do not fall. On BIRD dev, it is
also above FedDPO trained on the fixed execution-labeled pairs of its
own first preference round, under EX and TS. A preference round sends
what a FedAvg SFT round sends and adds client computation for sampling,
labeling and training. These results come from one training run per
model, with models of at most \setting{model.size} parameters, and from
comparisons at equal numbers of rounds in which the preference rounds
and the FedAvg SFT rounds use different optimizer settings
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Three directions follow from the scope of the study: federations with
partial participation and many clients, labels that are checked on
several contents of a client's database instead of one, and an analysis
of what the adapter and the pair count reveal about the data of a
client.
