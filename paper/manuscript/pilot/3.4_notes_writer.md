# Notes to the editor: Section 3.4, A preference round

Draft: `pilot/3.4_preference_round.tex`. Read for it: the guide,
`TERMS.md`, `voice/style_profile.md`, `voice/method.md`,
`voice/profile.md` and the brief. Nothing else.

## Length

About 775 words of running text by my own count, in which each inline
formula, each macro and each `Section~\ref` number is one word and the
three displayed equations are left out. A counter that drops inline
math gives about 715; one that splits inline math at spaces gives about
800. If the count of the tool is above 800, the two cheapest cuts are
the Spider counts (the clause after the semicolon in the on-policy
paragraph, 8 words) and "a centralized method" (3 words).

## Structure

Nine paragraphs, in the order of a round: purpose and step 1; sampling
and labeling; pair construction; questions without a pair; the loss;
the reference model and the NLL term; what follows CodeLutra and what
differs; on-policy pairs with the counts; upload and aggregation. The
steps are not numbered, since Algorithm 1 lists them.

## What the brief lacked

1. No reason for $M$ = 2 where CodeLutra constructs 10, and none for
   averaging the NLL term over tokens. Both are stated as differences
   without a reason. A reviewer will ask for the first; one sentence
   would do if the reason is the cost of local training (Section 3.5).
2. No reason for leaving a truncated sample unlabeled, and none for
   sampling without top-$p$ or top-$k$ truncation. Both are stated as
   facts.
3. Why it matters that the reference model is identical for all
   clients. The text states the property and draws nothing from it.
4. Whether a question with an empty database, or with a gold SQL query
   that fails to execute, is sampled again in the next round. The text
   says "sampled again" only for the first case (all samples of one
   label), as in statement 2.
5. No label for Appendix B. The time limit is named without a pointer
   and without a value.
6. Whether `\setting` and `\fact` can be used in math mode. Every macro
   is in text mode; the values are given as "we set $N$ to ...".

## Sentences that go beyond the wording of the brief

Each follows from a fact of the brief; check that you agree.

- "The NLL term maintains the likelihood of the correct sample, which
  the preference term constrains only relative to that of the incorrect
  sample." The second clause is read from the formula of $m_\theta$. It
  is the motivation of the NLL term; the brief gives the effect only.
- "pairs constructed once would reflect those of the model that
  generated them". A logical contrast, not a measured one. FedDPO is
  not named here, since the brief does not say how its pairs are
  constructed.
- "FedAvg weights a client by its amount of local data: $n_k$ in a
  warm-up round, the number of pairs here." Joins the fact on FedAvg
  with the two weights of the brief.
- "The draw is over the samples rather than over the distinct queries."
  Replaces the example of the brief (a query generated three times is
  three times as likely) to save words.
- The opening sentence says that a preference round "trains it to
  prefer, among its own samples, those that reproduce the execution
  result of the gold SQL query on the client's database". It names the
  gold SQL query, as the ceiling of statement 1 requires.

## Departures and choices

- The numbers of pairs (11,754 and 7,066; 8,026 and 3,354) are not
  printed: each is $M$ times the number of questions with pairs, which
  is printed. The fact that every such question yields exactly $M$
  pairs is not stated separately; it follows from the construction.
- The number of BIRD questions whose samples are all incorrect is not
  mentioned, since it has no macro. The text gives the growth of the
  all-correct questions and "mostly".
- $m_\theta$ has no name in the text; `TERMS.md` has none for it. The
  label of its equation is `eq:logratio`. The other labels are
  `eq:loss` and `eq:aggregation`.
- CodeLutra's 10 pairs is written as the word "ten": it is a fact of a
  cited paper and has no macro.
- "negative log-likelihood (NLL)" is given in full here, on the guess
  that this is its first use. DPO and FedAvg are used as abbreviations,
  on the guess that Sections 1 to 3.3 introduce them. "Execution
  accuracy" is written in full and not abbreviated.
- `\setting{pref.epochs} epoch` has the singular typed after the macro.
- "A sample that contains no SQL query" where the brief says "an
  output": `TERMS.md` rules out "output" for a sample.

## Doubts

- The CodeLutra paragraph has two sentences. It could close the
  paragraph before it, which would then have eight sentences and two
  points.
- Section 6 is pointed to twice (the two terms, the on-policy pairs).
  One pointer at the end of the on-policy paragraph would save a line
  but would stand in the wrong paragraph for the terms.
- "the size of the upload ... equals that of a warm-up round" follows
  the brief. The upload also carries one integer, the number of pairs;
  the text does not count it.

## Proposals

- A small worked example, as a figure or a boxed listing: one training
  question, its gold SQL query, a few of its samples with their
  execution labels (one correct, one execution error, one incorrect
  result) and the two pairs constructed from them. It would define the
  labels and the pair by example and would let the labeling paragraph
  lose a sentence.
- A small table of the counts by preference round (questions with
  pairs, all correct, all incorrect, pairs) for BIRD and Spider, here
  or in Section 3.5. It needs macros for the all-incorrect counts and
  would replace the sentence of counts in the on-policy paragraph.
