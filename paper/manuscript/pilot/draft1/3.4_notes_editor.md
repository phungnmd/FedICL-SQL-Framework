## 2. What the writer could not know (the packet is at fault)

1. Heading levels. Neither the role nor the packet says that nothing
   below `\subsubsection` exists.
2. A macro that prints its unit. The packet showed "(73.9 MB)" and did
   not say the unit is inside the macro.
3. Step 6 of the round said the client sends the adapter "and nothing
   else".
4. The time limit of a query was not given (BIRD: 30 s for the gold SQL
   and the sample together; Spider: 60 s per query).
5. How the pairs of one question are drawn (finding above).
6. The row on CodeLutra did not say that its reference model is also
   the model that was sampled, or how its second term is normalised.
7. Counts by question, which the writer asked for:

   | | Round | Questions | With a pair | All samples correct | All samples wrong | Gold SQL does not run |
   |---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | BIRD | first preference round | 9,428 | 5,877 | 416 | 2,698 | 437 |
   | BIRD | second | 9,428 | 3,533 | 3,016 | 2,437 | 442 |
   | Spider | first | 8,659 | 4,013 | 2,891 | 573 | 3 |
   | Spider | second | 8,659 | 1,677 | 5,784 | 628 | 3 |

   On Spider 1,759 questions are on databases without rows and give no
   pair in either round; they are counted in their own columns above
   and not in "with a pair". The questions that stop giving pairs are
   the ones the model now answers correctly in all ten samples; the
   count of questions it gets wrong in all ten hardly moves. These
   counts are in the committed round records and are not yet macros.

## 4. Register (added after an outside review of the draft)

A reviewer found the draft closer to a spoken explanation than to a
paper, with repeated words and loose terms. Measured against the method
sections of five papers whose sources are at hand, the finding holds
for the vocabulary and not for the sentences or the structure:

| Text | Words per sentence | Letters per word | Words of 9+ letters | Everyday verbs per 1,000 words |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Draft 3.4 | 20.9 | 4.44 | 4.0% | 39.2 |
| CodeLutra, Section 3 | 20.6 | 5.82 | 22.3% | 0.0 |
| GradeSQL (the target journal), Methodology | 23.1 | 5.64 | 19.9% | 6.0 |
| CodeS (SIGMOD 2024), method | 22.3 | 5.75 | 17.4% | 1.7 |
| DPO (NeurIPS 2023), Section 4 | 26.5 | 5.64 | 18.7% | 0.0 |
| FedAvg (AISTATS 2017), Section 2 | 29.2 | 5.26 | 14.2% | 4.3 |

Everyday verbs counted: write, run, give, get, say, keep, leave, hold,
let, teach, and "right", "built", "cut". Word length is a rough
measure; the gap is far outside the spread of the five papers.

What is loose in the terms:

- two words for one thing: "right" and "correct"; "query", "sample"
  and "output" for the sampled SQL;
- the object of a verb is the sample where it is its likelihood: "has
  raised the correct sample", "the difference between the two
  samples", "keeps the likelihood of the correct sample";
- an everyday phrase where the field has a term: "lets the model
  write" (sampling), "does not run" (fails to execute), "how far the
  model may move from the reference" (the strength of the constraint
  to the reference model), "gets no label", "cut off".

Repeated or roundabout, three sentences of 45: "with results compared
as the execution accuracy of the benchmark compares them"; "What
leaves it is what leaves it in a FedAvg SFT round"; "A preference
round uses what a client holds beyond it: the database". "Gives no
pair" stands three times in one paragraph.

What holds: the idea comes before each formula and its meaning after
it, terms are defined before use, no claim goes past its evidence,
no word of praise, and every statement checked is true.

Where it comes from: 37% of the draft's words stand in runs of five or
more words taken from the packet or the role file, and the phrases
above are among them ("gets no label", "gives no pair", "many right
and wrong queries of the model's own", "how far the model may move").
The packet and the role are written in the plain words of the lab
notes, the role asks to claim "plainly", and the writer used the
packet's sentences as text. The seven voice passages did not outweigh
that.

Proposed (each needs the owner's yes):

- `WRITER.md`: a paragraph on register. The vocabulary is that of the
  field, as in the voice passages; the packet states facts in the
  lab's shorthand and none of its sentences is text for the paper; one
  term for one thing.
- Term sheet: the field's terms beside the lab's (sample or candidate
  query; correct and incorrect; execute; generate), and one of each
  pair chosen.
- Packet format: facts as tables and short entries, not as sentences
  that can be lifted.
- Voice folder: the method section of CodeLutra and of GradeSQL in
  `method.md`; they describe the same kind of steps as this section.
