## 1. Findings

**F1. Major. §2.2, Liu et al.: "falls for six when the output is the query alone"**
- The section reports that DPO without chain-of-thought lowered EX on BIRD dev for six of ten models.
- §3.1 then says the model "outputs the SQL query alone", and Fig. A1 asks for "only the final SQL query".
- At this point I expected the authors to explain why their method avoids this result. They don't.
- Two explanations seem possible: the likelihood term taken from CodeLutra, or pairs renewed in every round instead of built once. Neither is stated.
- As written, the section quotes the main evidence against its own design and moves on.

**F2. Major. Table 1 and §2.3, PubSwap and Fed-GRPO: "apply reinforcement learning at the clients with a reward for a correct answer … code generation"**
- These are the nearest federated works: training on the model's own outputs, with a reward that for code is presumably test execution on the client.
- Table 1 leaves them out but includes FedDPO, which has neither execution nor Text-to-SQL. Liu et al. (Text-to-SQL, execution-labelled DPO) are also left out.
- The novelty claim in §2.4 ("no earlier method does so with execution labels and pairs of the model's own outputs constructed anew") is true because "pairs" excludes GRPO-style methods by definition.
- §2.1 lists execution-reward RL for Text-to-SQL, and §2.3 lists federated RL with verifiable rewards. Together they make "federated GRPO for Text-to-SQL" the obvious alternative, and the section gives no reason to prefer DPO pairs over it.

**F3. Major. §2.2: "FedLS-SQL follows it in its sampling and loss settings" plus the three "only the client has the database" sentences**
- From Section 2 the contribution reads as CodeLutra placed inside FedAvg.
- The only gap the section names is that execution needs the database and only the client has it. Yet in the setting of §3.1 the client holds the database, the gold SQL and the model, so it can run CodeLutra locally.
- Nothing here says what becomes hard or different when the two are combined: client drift in the pairs, the reference model across rounds, heterogeneity, or cost of sampling on the client.
- A reviewer will ask what the method contributes beyond the combination.

**F4. Minor. §2.3, "In the FedDPO of OpenFedLLM [35], one of our baselines" vs. Table 1 (FedDPO: Text-to-SQL ×, execution ×)**
- As a baseline, FedDPO must run on Text-to-SQL with some pairs.
- Those pairs are probably execution-labelled and built once from a fixed model. If so, the baseline is closer to "federated Liu et al." than to the FedDPO row in Table 1.
- I could not tell which object the table describes or which one is being compared against.

**F5. Minor. §2.1, last paragraph: "Data privacy, one of the motives of CodeS, belongs to this setting … cannot pool them"**
- The section does not say why several organizations would want a joint model at all.
- §2.1 itself describes strong open models (OmniSQL on public synthetic data) that one organization could fine-tune locally. Related work is where the reader expects the gap between "local fine-tuning of an open model" and "federated training" to be argued, and it isn't.

**F6. Minor. §2.3, first paragraph: "each client trains an adapter … and the server averages the adapters"**
- The aggregation is presented as unproblematic. Averaging the LoRA A and B matrices separately does not average the products, and there is a body of work on federated LoRA aggregation; none of it is mentioned.
- References [9], [18] and [19] (non-IID and FedProx) are in the list but not in Section 2.
- §2.4 reports a 30-point gap between FedAvg and centralized training (40.03 vs 70.32) and a fix (Lorar). The section never says whether FedLS-SQL addresses that gap or is orthogonal to it.

**F7. Minor. §2.1, opening: "whose methods differ in what the model is trained on"**
- This organizing principle fits CodeS, OmniSQL and SPFT-SQL. It breaks for SLM-SQL, Think2SQL and Arctic, which differ in the training objective.
- Parameter ranges are given for some methods ("the first of them for models of 0.5B to 1.5B") without saying why. The size of the paper's own backbone appears nowhere on these pages, so the sizes have nothing to be compared with.

**F8. Remark. §2.1, §2.2 and §2.3 endings**
- "All of these methods assume training at one site…", "In all works of this subsection, the party that trains holds…" and "None of the works … considers Text-to-SQL, where the label is the execution result on a database of the client" make the same point three times. §2.4 makes it a fourth time.

**F9. Remark. §2.2, CodeLutra: "which trains a Text-to-SQL model"**
- CodeLutra is a general code-generation method that is evaluated on Text-to-SQL among other tasks. Table 1 inherits this narrower description.
- Similarly, in §2.4 "a prediction is correct only when it is the same string as the gold SQL" may be stricter than the normalized exact match used in [38]. This needs checking against [38].

**F10. Remark. §2.2 and §2.3: EX, SFT, NLL**
- These are used without expansion at first use. This is moot if the Introduction will define them.

## 3. What I did not believe, or found asserted without support

- "To our knowledge, the work of Zhang et al. is the only earlier one that trains a Text-to-SQL model with federated learning." This is a strong claim, and the search behind it is not described.
- The implied claim that renewing pairs helps for Text-to-SQL. The support is from mathematics ([14]) and general reasoning ([26]). The one Text-to-SQL DPO study cited ([20]) used pairs built once, and its query-only result is negative.
- "an execution label requires the database, and in federated training only the client has it." This is presented as an obstacle, but the section doesn't show why it is one (F3).
- "Data privacy, one of the motives of CodeS, belongs to this setting." This is an interpretation of CodeS's motive and is not argued.
