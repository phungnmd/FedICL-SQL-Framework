## 1. Findings

**1. Major: the table/column analysis of §5.4 is not discussed, though it bears most on the headline BIRD result.**
- **Where:** §7.2, "An execution label rests on one database… EX has the same limit on the evaluation database (Section 5.4). The check available is TS… BIRD has no test suite."
- **What it does to the reader:** §7 states this limit only in general terms. §5.4's own counts give it a size:
  - On BIRD dev, the correct predictions that read the gold tables and columns go from 517 to 557 (+40). Those that read other tables or columns go from 136 to 191 (+55).
  - So more than half of the net gain of 95 questions is in the group whose correctness EX confirms only on the one evaluation database.
  - On Spider test the same split is +55 and +20 of 75. There, TS (Table 6) partly answers the concern.
- **Why it matters:** §7.1 ¶4 raises exactly this risk for the variant without warm-up: an execution label can accept a query that only matches the gold result on one database. It never asks whether the same risk applies to the BIRD gain of FedLS-SQL itself. Neither §7.2 nor §8 says that the 6.19-point result is the one with no stricter check.

**2. Major: the account of why the two preference rounds differ (§7.1 ¶2) is not supported, and the Spider data run against it.**
- **Where:** "on BIRD the difference is consistent with the incorrect samples of their pairs…"
- **Absolute counts undercut the shares.** The account is given in shares of pairs whose incorrect side is an execution error. In absolute numbers, the pairs whose incorrect side is an incorrect result are almost the same in both BIRD rounds: 11,754 − 6,616 = 5,138 and 7,066 − 1,788 = 5,278. Yet incorrect results rise by 130 in round 1 and fall by 65 in round 2.
- **A competing reading fits equally well.** What changes between the rounds is the number of execution-error pairs, which falls from 6,616 to 1,788. The rise in round 1 then looks like ¶1's conversion of execution errors into incorrect results, not like a property of the pair mix.
- **Spider goes the wrong way.** The share falls only from 51% to 43%, but the direction should be the same as on BIRD: fixed round-1 pairs should leave fewer execution errors than FedLS-SQL. They leave more (97 against 84). The text only says Spider "does not show the pattern", which understates this.
- **The fixed-pairs row does not separate the causes.** "Fixed pairs and reference" changes three things at once:
  - the composition of the pairs;
  - training a second epoch on the same pairs;
  - the older reference model.
  - "Further in the direction of the first" is what a second pass over the same data would give whatever the composition.
- **The evidence is thin.** There are two rounds, one seed, and every difference is untested ("values without a test").

**3. Major: the interpretation of the warm-up rounds (§7.1 ¶4) is weaker than written and leaves out an obvious confound.**
- **Where:** "their effect appears… under TS (Section 6)" and "One reading is that training first on the gold SQL query… limits what the model takes from such labels."
- **The TS evidence is limited.**
  - Against FedLS-SQL, the lower TS of the variant without warm-up is significant on Spider-Syn only (Table 6; p = 0.01 and 0.0067 do not survive the Holm correction).
  - Against FedAvg SFT, the TS deficits are 0.78, 0.49 and 1.77 points, all untested.
- **An alternative is not considered.** The variant without warm-up has four preference rounds instead of two. More rounds of training on execution labels is an equally available explanation for its EX–TS gap.
- **The reading is not tied to any number.** The text could cite, for example, the EX−TS gap in Table 6: 10.06 for the variant against 7.25 for FedLS-SQL on Spider dev. It could also cite the share of training samples labeled correct that differ from the gold query.
- **A relevant result is ignored.** The §6 variant that pairs the gold SQL as the correct side (39.24 against 41.85 EX; execution errors 219→105, incorrect results 673→827) bears directly on "training first on the gold SQL". §7 does not mention it.

**4. Minor: results appear for the first time in the Discussion.**
- **Where:** §7.1 ¶2. Several numbers are in no table, figure or earlier section:
  - 6,616 of 11,754 pairs; 51%; 1,788 of 7,066 pairs; 43%;
  - the split of the fixed-pairs row into execution errors and incorrect results: 91/735 on BIRD and 97/375 on Spider.
- **Spider is reported only as percentages.** The reader cannot check those values against Table 7.

**5. Minor: the role of the NLL term (§7.1 ¶3) is restated, not interpreted, and its mechanism is asserted without measurement.**
- **Where:** "the preference term constrains the two samples of a pair only relative to each other (Section 3.4)".
- **Restatement:** the paragraph repeats §6 almost sentence by sentence, including the CodeLutra / Pang et al. citation.
- **Unmeasured mechanism:** the closing clause repeats §3.4's rationale as if it explained the Spider result. No measurement shows that the likelihood of y⁺ falls without the NLL term.
- **A comparison is not drawn:** a round of NLL on the model's own correct samples (+1.69 / +1.77 points) and a third round of FedAvg SFT on the gold queries (+0.98 / +0.61 points).

**6. Minor: §5.4's pointer "Section 7 returns to them" is answered by a restatement and a truism.**
- **Where:** §7.1 ¶1, "The incorrect results do not decrease because… executing is necessary for a correct result and not sufficient."
- **Other flows are left out of the causal "because":**
  - the 58 questions that only FedAvg SFT answers correctly;
  - 42 of FedLS-SQL's 128 execution errors (128 − (268 − 75 − 107)) are on questions where FedAvg SFT did not fail to execute.
- **"No fewer" hides the direction.** The incorrect results are in fact more: 658 against 613 and 384 against 370.

**7. Minor: §7.2 leaves out limits that the earlier sections set up.**
- **The heterogeneity is narrow.** All clients come from one benchmark's training set, and the skew is in query complexity only (§4.2).
- **The integer a client uploads reveals more than in FedAvg.** |P_k^t| changes from round to round with how the model performs on the client's data. The n_k of FedAvg does not, yet the introduction presents the two as equivalent. §6 shows the weighting can be replaced at no measurable cost.
- **The cost is reported per round only.**
  - Client time is summed over clients run one after another on one GPU.
  - The total over training is never stated: on BIRD, 1,207 against 692 minutes, about 1.7×.
  - Running vLLM and query execution at each client goes beyond plain "computation".

**8. Minor: the "T_w = 2… gain is smaller with 0.5B" sentence (§7.2) implies a cause it does not state or support.**
- Placing the two facts side by side suggests the fixed T_w explains the smaller gain at 0.5B. Table 8 shows T_w makes no EX difference on Spider for the 1.5B model, and nothing was run at 0.5B.

**9. Remark: the method summary in §8 leaves out the NLL term.**
- §8 says the clients train "on preference pairs of a correct and an incorrect sample". §7.1 shows the preference term alone brings no gain on Spider.
- §8 also gives the Spider gain without a number (3.49).

**10. Remark: the hedging labels "(values without a test)" and "a comparison of values" have to be decoded.** They mark the weakest claims of §7.1 and still sit inside sentences that draw conclusions from them.
