## 1. Findings (most important first)

**F1. The "FedDPO" baseline is not FedDPO. The gain over it mixes three changes. (Major)**
- **Where:** lines 79–81, "Following the published protocol, we fix the execution-labeled pairs and reference model from FedExec-SQL's first preference round and optimize the DPO loss alone"; Abstract lines 16–17.
- **What happened to me:** Line 50 and the Abstract (l. 6–7) say FedDPO trains on *external* pairs. The baseline instead trains on FedExec-SQL's own execution-labeled, model-generated pairs. Only the fixed pairs and fixed reference follow the published protocol.
- So the baseline is really an ablation of FedExec-SQL. It differs in three ways at once: no pair renewal, no reference renewal, and no NLL term. The +3.26 EX and +3.98 TS gains can't be credited to any one of them.
- Calling it "FedDPO" in the Abstract makes the comparison look like one against a published method.
- It is run on BIRD only, and the text never says why it is missing on Spider.

**F2. One training run per method; significance covers only test-set sampling. (Major)**
- **Where:** lines 75–76, "Holm-adjusted p-values from exact McNemar tests on paired single-run predictions."
- **What happened to me:** McNemar on paired predictions tests whether two *fixed* models differ on these questions. It says nothing about variance across training seeds, client sampling or temperature-1 pair sampling.
- With one run, a +3.26-point gap over the fixed-pair baseline could fall within seed-to-seed variation. "Both gains are significant after Holm correction" (l. 83) claims more than the test supports.
- The Holm family is never defined, so I can't tell which comparisons were corrected together.
- No p-value is given for the TS gain over the baseline (l. 83) or for the 4.89-point TS gain (l. 99).

**F3. The headline comparison is made at a round where the baseline has already declined. (Major)**
- **Where:** lines 84–86, "FedAvg SFT stops improving after round three on both benchmarks… its round-five Spider test score by +4.28."
- **What happened to me:** The arithmetic puts FedAvg SFT's BIRD peak at 43.68%. At round 4 it is 42.57%. So the Abstract's +6.19 is measured against a baseline already past its peak; the fair figure is +5.08.
- On Spider, the text says the peak is at round 3, but the comparison is made against round 5 (73.92%), not the peak. The peak value is never given. That gap could be smaller than +3.49.
- Why 4 rounds total (2 warm-up + 2 preference) is never justified. FedExec-SQL beyond round 4 is not mentioned.

**F4. An obvious competing baseline is not discussed. (Major)**
- **Where:** lines 52–54, "Centralized Text-to-SQL methods exploit execution feedback … on pooled data [12–15], whereas under federation only each client holds its databases."
- **What happened to me:** I expected an argument for why a centralized execution-feedback method can't simply run locally inside FedAvg. Examples: rejection-sampling SFT on execution-correct samples, or a GRPO-style update with an execution reward. None comes.
- FedExec-SQL looks close to "local execution-filtered DPO + SFT, averaged". Its NLL term on correct samples is itself rejection-sampling fine-tuning.
- Without that argument, or such a baseline, the novelty claim at l. 92–93 ("the first federated training method for Text-to-SQL that exploits it") rests on framing.

**F5. "Halve execution errors" hides that incorrect results rise, and the count order flips between sentences. (Minor)**
- **Where:** lines 86–88, "(268 to 128…)" and "(658 vs. 613…)", "(384 vs. 370…)".
- **What happened to me:** Line 87 lists FedAvg SFT first. Line 88 lists FedExec-SQL first. I only found this by reconstructing correct-answer counts:
  - BIRD (1534 questions): 268 + 613 = 881 wrong → 42.57%; 128 + 658 = 786 wrong → 48.76%.
  - Spider (2147 questions): 173 + 370 = 543 wrong; 84 + 384 = 468 wrong.
- So FedExec-SQL produces *more* incorrect results on both benchmarks (+45, +14). On BIRD, about a third of the removed execution errors became wrong answers rather than correct ones.
- "Do not differ significantly" is technically accurate but steers the reader away from this.

**F6. The TS statement in contribution 3 is unparseable, and it undercuts the training signal. (Minor; would ask a question)**
- **Where:** lines 98–104, "a 4.89-point TS gain on a generated BIRD dev suite… queries using other tables or columns than the gold SQL query account for much of the TS gain (132 vs. 86), after the suite rejects 30.9% of our EX-correct queries in this group."
- **What happened to me:**
  - The baseline for the 4.89 points is not named. I inferred FedAvg SFT, at a TS of 36.70%, which appears nowhere.
  - "Generated" suite appears here for the first time, although TS was already used at l. 82–83.
  - I couldn't tell whether 132 vs. 86 are TS-correct counts and for which models.
  - Substantively: if 30.9% of EX-correct queries that use different tables or columns are spurious, then the single-database result match used to *label training samples* (Fig. 1) is noisy in the same way. The Introduction doesn't address this.

**F7. "On-policy, renewed every round" rests on one renewal. (Minor)**
- **Where:** Abstract l. 10–11; lines 67–69, "so every preference round forms one resampling step"; l. 95–96.
- **What happened to me:** There are only 2 preference rounds, so pairs and reference are renewed exactly once. The emphasis on per-round renewal is larger than the design can show.
- Within a round, the local model drifts away from the global model that generated the samples. "On-policy" holds only at the start of each round.

**F8. Figure 1 does not show how pairs are built. (Minor)**
- **Where:** caption, "where two distinct correct samples with identical SQL text (first row) are paired against the incorrect result and the execution error."
- **What happened to me:** I read this three times. The figure shows one row (SQL 1) used in both pairs. The caption says these are two different samples with the same text.
- The selection rule is not stated. I can't tell how correct and incorrect samples are chosen for the M = 2 pairs, whether duplicate texts are allowed, or what happens with fewer than M incorrect samples or with all-correct questions.
- Line 64 mentions only questions with "only incorrect samples" yielding no pair. All-correct questions also yield none, and the example (8 of 10 correct) suggests these may be common. The share of questions that yield pairs is not given.

**F9. Privacy and communication claims go beyond what is shown. (Remark)**
- **Where:** Abstract l. 18–20; l. 58–59, "while all private data remain inside the client boundary"; l. 97, "its integer weight".
- **What happened to me:** "Integer weight" is never defined. If it is the number of pairs rather than n_k, it reveals per-round accuracy of each client.
- Adapter updates can leak information. No formal privacy claim is made, yet the wording implies one.
- "Unchanged communication cost" is true. But sampling 10 queries per question plus execution adds client compute that the Introduction doesn't size; it only points to Section 5.6.

**F10. Unsupported framing and wording slips. (Remark)**
- l. 30: "SLMs of 0.5 billion to 1.5 billion parameters" is an arbitrary range, and only 1.5B is tested.
- l. 39–40: [7] (FedAvg) is cited for federated LoRA frameworks.
- l. 41: "existing federated preference alignment" is represented by FedDPO alone.
- l. 106: the code release promise has no link.
- Template remnants: "2026submitted" in the running header; the license line reads "for under a Creative Commons… which permitted."
