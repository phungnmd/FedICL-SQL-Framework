## Response

# Review of Sections 7 (Discussion and limitations) and 8 (Conclusion)

I read the earlier pages once for context, then read Sections 7–8 from start to end. I checked every number in Sections 7–8 against Sections 5–6, Fig. 3 and Tables 3, 6, 7 and 8. The arithmetic is consistent throughout: the error counts add up to the EX values, the pair counts add up to Table 7, and the cost ratios match Table 7. The problems are in interpretation, not in the numbers.

**Where I stopped while reading:**
- 7.1 ¶2, at "107 of its execution errors".
- 7.1 ¶2, at "What the preference term adds through the incorrect samples".
- 7.1 ¶3, at the second-round counts. I went back to §5.4 to see what happened to the incorrect results.
- 7.1 ¶4, at "is a comparison of values".
- 7.2 ¶1, at the sentence on pair counts and weights.
- 7.2 ¶2, at "136 to 191". I stopped to work out what share of the BIRD gain this is.
- 8 ¶2, at the second direction.

## 1. Findings

**1. Most of the BIRD gain is in predictions that only EX checks, and Sections 7–8 draw no consequence from this. (Major)**
- **Where:** 7.2 ¶2, "those that read other tables or columns from 136 to 191 … for the latter, EX is the only check".
- **What happened:**
  - Between FedAvg SFT and FedLS-SQL, the net gain in correct predictions on BIRD dev is 95 (653 → 748).
  - 55 of those 95 (58%) are in the "other tables or columns" group. Only 40 are in the group that reads the gold tables and columns.
  - The share of the "other" group among correct predictions rises from 20.8% to 25.5%.
  - On Spider the split is the reverse: 55 in the gold-table group and 20 in the other group.
- **Why it matters:** BIRD is the main dataset and supplies the headline 6.19 points.
  - 7.1 ¶4 proposes exactly this failure mode: execution labels favour queries that return the gold result on one database without being equivalent to the gold query.
  - But 7.1 applies that reading only to the variant without warm-up rounds, never to FedLS-SQL on BIRD.
  - Section 8 then repeats "by 6.19 points of EX" without any qualification.
- **Also:** the "other" group lumps together supersets (for example an extra join), subsets and substitutions. So the count by itself does not say how many of these predictions are wrong.

**2. The NLL-only result undercuts the claimed contribution, and the Discussion does not follow it through. (Major)**
- **Where:** 7.1 ¶2, "With the NLL term alone on the correct samples, a round ends above the model before it in EX on both sets … a difference in EX to the round with both terms is not established."
- **What happened:**
  - Taken together with Table 8, this says one round of NLL on execution-filtered samples gives 44.39 on BIRD and 75.87 on Spider.
  - Both are above a third round of FedAvg SFT (43.68 and 74.71 in §5.1). Both are comparisons of values only, not tested.
  - The preference term's own contribution to EX is not significant on either set.
- **Why it matters:**
  - On these pages, the case for pairs plus DPO over federated self-training on correct samples rests on execution-error counts, not on EX.
  - The NLL-only variant was run for one round only, so it is unknown whether it matches FedLS-SQL at T = 4.
  - The Conclusion describes the method and its gain as if both terms had been shown to be needed for the EX gain.
  - Neither section relates this to the earlier observation from CodeLutra and Pang et al. on the NLL term, or to self-training baselines in general.

**3. "No such effect is established for the incorrect results" contradicts its own paragraph. (Minor)**
- **Where:** 7.1 ¶1.
- **What happened:** The same paragraph reports that on BIRD dev the incorrect results are *significantly more numerous* with the preference term (723 vs 605, p = 1.0×10⁻¹²). An effect is established; it runs in the adverse direction.
- **Also:** the opening sentence attributes the execution-error effect to "FedLS-SQL". The only tested evidence is the one-round ablation of the *preference term*. The halving relative to FedAvg SFT (128 vs 268, 84 vs 173) is not tested.

**4. Fewer execution errors is presented as a benefit without noting that part of it is a shift to silent failures. (Minor)**
- **Where:** 7.1 ¶1–2, and Section 8: "The execution errors of its predictions fall by about half".
- **What happened:** Of the 268 execution errors of FedAvg SFT on BIRD:
  - 75 become correct;
  - 107 become executable queries that return a wrong result.
- **Why it matters:** For a deployed system, a wrong result that executes is a silent failure, arguably worse than an error. The abstract, the Discussion and the Conclusion all present the halving as a gain in itself.

**5. The account of the preference pairs is selective in the second round. (Minor)**
- **Where:** 7.1 ¶3, "the round no longer lowers the execution errors on BIRD dev".
- **What happened:**
  - On BIRD, execution errors *rise* (102 → 128, §5.4). "No longer lowers" understates this.
  - The paragraph also omits that incorrect results *fall* in that round (723 → 658), when 75% of the pairs (5,278 of 7,066) have an incorrect result as their incorrect side.
- **Why it matters:** That observation bears directly on the claim at the end of ¶2 that the preference term acts only on execution errors.
- **Also:** the breakdown of pairs by type (6,616 / 5,138 and so on) appears for the first time in the Discussion. It has no table and no test, and I could not tell whether ¶3 means that the second round agrees with "this account" or not.

**6. The warm-up paragraph omits an obvious alternative and the method's own rationale. (Minor)**
- **Where:** 7.1 ¶4.
- **What happened:**
  - The variant with T_w = 0 never trains on a gold SQL query as a target. That alone could explain a lower TS, and it is not mentioned.
  - The rationale given in §3.3 for warm-up rounds is that fewer questions lack a correct sample (4,035 vs 2,698 in §6). It is not revisited.
  - The "one reading" is untested, although the table/column analysis of §5.4 could be applied to the variant.
  - The BIRD row for the variant is still pending, so this evidence is from Spider only. The paragraph says this only indirectly ("narrower").

**7. Limitations that are missing from 7.2. (Minor)**
- **Number of rounds:** only two preference rounds (T = 4) are studied. On BIRD, execution errors already rise in the second round, and nothing is said about longer training.
- **How settings were chosen:** T_w = 2, T = 4 and M = 2 are called "a setting". The text does not say how they were chosen, or whether BIRD dev, which is the only BIRD evaluation set, was consulted.
- **Unsafe execution:** model-sampled SQL is executed on the clients' private, internal databases. Sandboxing, read-only access, write statements and load are not discussed. Labelling alone takes 159–168 minutes per round on BIRD.

**8. Inconsistent description of the federated setups. (Remark)**
- **Where:** 7.2 ¶1, "skewed by query complexity only … more clients and other heterogeneity are not studied".
- **What happened:**
  - §4.2 says the clients differ in domain and schema in every setup, and Table 2 shows they also differ in size.
  - One setup is IID, with no complexity skew.
  - A ten-client setup exists, so "more clients … not studied" needs a bound, for example "more than ten".

**9. The privacy sentence offers an untested mitigation without saying so. (Remark)**
- **Where:** 7.2 ¶1, "with question counts as weights, EX does not differ significantly (Section 6)".
- **What happened:**
  - The sentence implies that weighting by question count is a way to avoid revealing pair counts, but does not say so.
  - That alternative was tested after one preference round only (the 3-round block of Table 8), not at T = 4.
- **Also:** gold queries that fail to execute (350 on BIRD train) also yield no pair, and 7.2 ¶2 leaves them out.

**10. Conclusion. (Remark)**
- "The clients use their databases as supervision": the supervision is still the gold SQL query; the databases turn it into labels.
- The Spider gain is given without a number, while the BIRD gain has one.
- The second direction, "questions for which a client has no gold SQL query", conflicts with the labelling rule (§3.4), which needs the gold result. No hint is given of how it would work.
- Section 7 does not place its findings against related work, for example Liu et al. on DPO without chain-of-thought, or CodeLutra.

## 2. What I could not reproduce without guessing

- **Pair breakdown:** whether the "execution error" counts of the pairs in 7.1 ¶3 include time-outs, as §3.4 defines them. I would also need to know whether they are counted per pair, so that a question's incorrect sample drawn twice counts twice.
- **Table/column grouping:** the "same tables and columns" vs "other" classification on BIRD. Unspecified: the parser's handling of aliases, `*`, and columns used only in WHERE or ORDER BY, and whether a superset counts as "other".
- **Significance tests on error counts:** how they were run (McNemar on a per-question binary indicator?), and in which Holm family they sit. 7.1 says "significantly" on that basis.
- **The NLL-only variant:** which correct samples it trains on. It appears to be only the y⁺ of the pairs, so only questions with both labels and with possible duplicates, rather than all correct samples. This matters for what Finding 2 can conclude.
- **Cost ratio:** "0.9 and 2.7 times" is a ratio of time summed over five clients run one after another. The per-client ratio and the wall-clock time of a parallel round are not given.
- **Settings:** how T, T_w and M were chosen (Finding 7).

## 3. What I did not believe, or found unsupported

- 7.1 ¶1 "No such effect is established": an adverse effect is established (Finding 3).
- 7.1 ¶1 "the results establish an effect of FedLS-SQL": the comparison with FedAvg SFT is untested; the test is on the preference term after one round.
- 7.1 ¶2 "The gain in EX nevertheless comes from both kinds": this counts the 153 questions gained and ignores the 58 lost, whose kinds are not given.
- 7.1 ¶3 "The preference pairs agree with this account": this holds only for round 1, and the round-2 evidence is reported selectively (Finding 5).
- 7.1 ¶4 "One reading is that training on execution labels from the first round favors queries…": no evidence is given, and the same reading is not applied to FedLS-SQL on BIRD (Findings 1 and 6).
- 8 "the clients use their databases as supervision": overstated (Finding 10).

## 4. Disagreements between the text, tables, figures and earlier parts

- **7.1 ¶3 vs §5.4 and Fig. 3:** "no longer lowers the execution errors" vs "the execution errors rise to 128".
- **7.1 ¶1 internally and vs §6:** "No such effect" vs a significant increase in incorrect results.
- **7.2 ¶1 vs §4.2 and Table 2:** "skewed by query complexity only" vs clients that differ in domain, schema and size, plus an IID setup. "More clients … not studied" vs the ten-client setup.
- **§5.4 vs Section 7:** §5.4 says "Section 7 returns to them" (the incorrect results). Section 7 restates the counts and adds no explanation beyond the 107 conversions already given in §5.4.
- **§3.3 vs 7.1 ¶4:** §3.3 justifies warm-up rounds by the number of questions without a correct sample; 7.1 ¶4 discusses warm-up rounds only through TS.
- **7.2 ¶2 vs §3.4 and §4.2:** 7.2 lists only databases without rows as yielding no pair; the earlier sections also list gold queries that fail to execute.
- **8 vs 7.1–7.2:** the Conclusion states the 6.19-point gain and the halving of execution errors. It omits the qualifications in 7.1 (the preference term's EX contribution is not established) and in 7.2 (EX is the only check for the "other" group on BIRD).
