## 1. Findings

**1. Lines 86–88, "The counts of incorrect results are comparable … (658 vs. 613 …) and Spider test (384 vs. 370 …)". Major.**
- Your EX figures give, for BIRD, FedAvg SFT 653 correct = 1,534 − 268 − 613, and FedExec-SQL 748 correct = 1,534 − 128 − 658.
- For Spider, FedAvg SFT 1,604 correct = 2,147 − 173 − 370, and FedExec-SQL 1,679 correct = 2,147 − 84 − 384.
- So FedExec-SQL produces *more* wrong-result queries on both benchmarks. Line 87 lists FedExec-SQL first, while line 86 ("268 to 128") lists FedAvg SFT first. I only found the direction by doing the arithmetic.
- The real finding is that part of the removed execution errors turn into wrong results, and all of the EX gain comes from fewer execution errors. Calling this "comparable" hides it, and the text needs to say it outright.

**2. Line 84, "its round-five Spider test score by +4.28". Major.**
- Line 82 says FedAvg SFT "stops improving after round three on both benchmarks". So the Spider peak is at round three, yet the comparison is against round five.
- Round five works out to 78.20 − 4.28 = 73.92, which is below FedAvg SFT's own round-four 74.71. The choice of comparison point looks selective.
- "its" in lines 83–84 grammatically refers to FedExec-SQL but means FedAvg SFT.

**3. Sections 3.2, 3.4 and 3.6: how many questions actually produce training pairs is never reported. Major.**
- 2,698 of 9,428 BIRD questions (28.6%) are all-incorrect after warm-up. These are presumably the hardest questions, and in preference rounds they get no gradient at all, whereas FedAvg SFT keeps training on them.
- The 62.3% at line 153 still includes empty-database questions, which 3.6 later excludes. So the share of questions that yield pairs is below 62.3%, and by how much is not stated.
- The number of qualifying questions and |P_k^t| per client per round are never given. These counts are the training set, they set the aggregation weights, and they determine the number of gradient steps.
- The claim that one annotation becomes "multi-sample supervision" (lines 145–146) does not hold for roughly 4 in 10 questions per round.

**4. Lines 194–197 and 204–205: BIRD timeout. Minor.**
- Line 194 says the gold y⋆ is executed "once per question". Line 197 says y⋆'s execution time counts against a shared timeout "per sample".
- I cannot tell whether y⋆ is re-executed for each sample or whether a single measured time is reused.
- Either way, whether a sample is labeled at all, and how, depends on wall-clock timing and hardware. That makes the labels, and therefore P_k^t, hard to reproduce.

**5. Lines 223–228 and 211–212: "align one iteration of preference optimization … with one federated communication round". Minor.**
- With T = 4 and T_w = 2 there are exactly two preference rounds, so the pairs are renewed only once.
- Most of the iterative framing in 3.6 and 3.7 therefore rests on the difference between round 3 and round 4.
- It also means the FedDPO comparison (fixed round-3 pairs, no NLL term) differs from FedExec-SQL in only one round of pairs plus the NLL term.

**6. Line 144 contradicts 3.5: "partitions the queries … into correct and incorrect subsets". Minor.**
- Section 3.5 adds a third class, unlabeled samples.
- "Unlabeled questions" appears at lines 154 and 182 before 3.5 defines unlabeled samples, and "unlabeled question" itself is never defined.
- Line 175 says "Under the sampling protocol of Section 3.2", but the protocol is described in 3.5.

**7. Lines 214–217: excluding empty databases. Minor.**
- The reason given (distinct queries return identical empty results) only explains why *correct* labels are unreliable on an empty database.
- Execution errors on an empty database are still reliable negatives, so the exclusion throws away valid negatives without saying so.
- The same argument applies to databases that are nearly empty, which are not excluded.
- The number of affected questions is not given.

**8. Lines 263–268: weighting aggregation by |P_k^t|. Minor.**
- The justification ("proportional to local training instances") skips over the fact that |P_k^t| depends on how many questions the current global model *partly* solves at each client. Under non-IID splits, this shifts weight away from clients whose questions are mostly too hard or mostly already solved.
- |P_k^t| is a function of the private execution labels, and it is sent to the server every round. That sits uneasily with line 267 ("execution labels … remain local").
- Algorithm 1, line 18 is undefined if P_k^t is empty for every client.

**9. Lines 248–251 and Eqs. (3)–(4). Remark.**
- m_θ = 0 before the first step follows only from π_θ = π_ref at initialization. That the sampling model is the same model is irrelevant to this point.
- Eq. (3) is a β-scaled sum over tokens, while the NLL term is a token average with λ = 1. The two terms are on different scales, and no reason is given for λ = 1 at that scale.

**10. Repetition and length (details in item 5). Remark.**

## 3. What I did not believe, or found asserted without support

- That incorrect results are "comparable" (finding 1). Your own counts show they go up.
- "preference rounds halve execution errors" (line 85). This is a causal claim based on one training run per configuration, with stochastic sampling at temperature 1. McNemar's test covers variation across test questions, not across training runs.
- Lines 101–103: the suite rejects 30.9% of FedExec-SQL's EX-correct predictions in the "other tables or columns" group. That is a high false-positive rate for EX, and it weakens the EX gains in general. The corresponding rate for FedAvg SFT is not given.
- Lines 186–187: that T_w = 0 "already yields preference pairs". No count is given.
- Line 262: "per-round communication volume remains identical". This is true per uploading client, but clients with an empty P_k^t upload nothing.
- Line 232: that a fixed M "determine[s] the number of local gradient updates". It does not fix the number; the count still varies with the number of qualifying questions.
