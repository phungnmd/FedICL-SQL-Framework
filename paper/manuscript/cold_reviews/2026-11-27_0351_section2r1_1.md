## 1. Findings

**F1. The distinction from CodeLutra is not established. Major.**
- **Where:** §2.2, "FedLS-SQL takes its sampling and loss settings from CodeLutra … a federated round renews the pairs, with its global model as the reference model, rounds of FedAvg SFT come first, and the number of pairs and the scale of the NLL term change." Also §2.4, "no earlier method does so with execution labels and pairs of the model's own outputs constructed anew in each round."
- **What happened:** One sentence earlier, CodeLutra already "constructs them anew in every iteration." So the first listed difference is something the two methods share. The other differences are:
  - the reference model;
  - the SFT warm-up;
  - two hyperparameters.
- After this paragraph I read the method as "CodeLutra run inside FedAvg." The section never names a problem that appears when you federate CodeLutra and that FedLS-SQL solves.
- The novelty sentence in §2.4 doesn't help. "Does so" means "trains Text-to-SQL federatedly," and the only earlier work doing that (Zhang et al.) uses SFT. So the claim is true by construction.

**F2. Fed-GRPO and PubSwap are set aside only because of their domain. Major.**
- **Where:** §2.3, "None of the federated works of this subsection considers Text-to-SQL."
- **What happened:** Fed-GRPO already does federated, on-policy training with a checkable reward for code generation. That is closer to FedLS-SQL than FedDPO is, which is the one federated preference method that gets a baseline.
- The text gives no reason for choosing DPO pairs over a GRPO-style reward at the clients. It also doesn't say why Fed-GRPO, applied to Text-to-SQL with an execution reward, isn't the obvious baseline.
- Neither method appears in Table 1.

**F3. Iterative/online DPO with an NLL term is missing. Major.**
- **Where:** §2.2, which credits "a likelihood term on the correct samples to the DPO term" and renewing pairs to CodeLutra.
- **What happened:** Pang et al. (2024, *Iterative Reasoning Preference Optimization*, arXiv:2404.19733) already combine four of the elements:
  - the DPO + NLL loss;
  - pairs renewed every iteration;
  - correct/incorrect labels from a gold answer;
  - the previous iteration's model as the reference.
- The online/iterative DPO line more broadly is also uncited. Without it, the reader gets the wrong idea of where FedLS-SQL's ingredients come from, and F1 gets worse.

**F4. Choosing query-only output contradicts the evidence the section cites. Minor.**
- **Where:** §2.2, "falls for six when the output is the query alone. Our model also outputs the query alone, and Section 6 examines the same choices…"
- **What happened:** Right after citing evidence against DPO on query-only output, the text adopts query-only output without giving a reason. It also hints, without saying so, that renewing the pairs and adding the NLL term fix the problem.
- "The same choices" took me a second reading. Liu et al. made three choices (pairs built once, DPO loss alone, fixed reference), but only two are named as ablated.

**F5. The motivation in §2.1 has a gap. Minor here; it becomes major if the Introduction doesn't close it.**
- **Where:** §2.1, "All of these methods assume training at one site … We consider several such organizations, which cannot pool their data."
- **What happened:** OmniSQL-type models are released with open weights. A single organization can run one locally, so it never has to send data to a provider. That means "training at one site" is not a limitation for these organizations.
- The section doesn't say what a single organization lacks that federation supplies, for example in-domain examples or its own schemas.
- The step from CodeS's privacy motive (one organization versus an API provider) to "several such organizations" is a change of setting, not a continuation.

**F6. Table 1 is built so that only FedLS-SQL gets every check mark. Minor.**
- **Where:** Table 1 caption, "by the properties that separate them."
- **What happened:**
  - The table leaves out works the text itself discusses: Liu et al. (Text-to-SQL ✓, execution ✓, own outputs, fixed), Fed-GRPO and PubSwap.
  - It also leaves out the two properties the text actually uses to separate FedLS-SQL from FedDPO and Liu et al.: the reference model and the NLL term.
  - It is unclear whether the column "Text-to-SQL" means "designed for" or "evaluated on" (see §4 below on CodeLutra).

**F7. "What a client can learn from the databases it holds" overstates the method. Minor.**
- **Where:** §2.4, "Our question is the supervision itself: what a client can learn from the databases it holds." Also §2.2, "the preference requires no annotator."
- **What happened:** By §2.2's own description, a preference is decided by comparison with the result of the gold SQL, and §3.1 puts y⋆ in every Dₖ.
- The signal is gold SQL plus execution, not the databases. "No annotator" is also misleading, because the gold SQL is the annotation.
- I first read these lines as describing a setting without labels.

**F8. FedDPO as a baseline is underspecified and may coincide with an ablation. Minor.**
- **Where:** §2.3, "We run FedDPO on Text-to-SQL as a baseline."
- **What happened:** FedDPO, as the section describes it, assumes a preference dataset already exists. Text-to-SQL doesn't have one.
- If the pairs are built once from the SFT model, the baseline looks the same as FedLS-SQL with renewal and the NLL term both removed, which overlaps the announced ablation. I couldn't tell which it is.

**F9. Federated LoRA is presented as solved. Minor.**
- **Where:** §2.3, "each client trains an adapter on its own data with SFT, and the server averages the adapters."
- **What happened:** This is stated as how things are done, with no mention of a known issue: averaging A and B separately is not the same as averaging the low-rank updates.
- The work on this is not cited, for example FedIT, FFA-LoRA and FLoRA. It matters because FedLS-SQL exchanges only a LoRA adapter (§3.1).

**F10. The argument about where labeling happens leads nowhere. Remark.**
- **Where:** §2.2, "In each work that labels by execution, the labeling takes place at the site that trains, since the label requires the database; in federated training, only a client holds it."
- **What happened:** In federated learning, the client is the site that trains locally. So labeling at the client is the natural design, not an obstacle.
- I read the sentence twice and still couldn't tell what gap it argues for.
