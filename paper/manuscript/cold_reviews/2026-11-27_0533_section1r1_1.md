## 1. Findings

**1. The headline gain is measured against a baseline that has already declined, and the comparison axis favours the method. Major.**
- Paragraph 8 says: "After 4 rounds … 48.76% … against 42.57% for FedAvg SFT, a gain of 6.19 points". It then says FedAvg SFT's "highest EX on BIRD dev, 43.68% at that round, is 5.08 points below".
- So the 6.19 in the abstract and the introduction is the gap to a baseline that dropped between rounds 3 and 4. The gap to the baseline's best result is 5.08. The abstract reports only 6.19.
- The axis is "the same number of rounds". Rounds measure communication, but each FedLS-SQL round also samples several queries per question, executes them, and runs a reference model. The text admits "the additional cost … is computation at the clients" but gives no number for it.
- There are only two preference rounds. A reader cannot tell whether FedLS-SQL keeps improving, levels off, or declines as the baseline did.

**2. The introduction never says what problem the federated setting creates that the design solves. Major.**
- Paragraph 6 sets up a difficulty: "the model whose queries are to be labeled is the global model, which changes with every round". Paragraph 7 answers it only by resampling every round.
- The stated differences from CodeLutra are that the loop runs "across the clients", runs "after the warm-up rounds", and uses the global model as reference. Each is described, but none is motivated by a federated difficulty such as client heterogeneity or uneven pair counts.
- As written, the method reads as CodeLutra placed inside FedAvg. The novelty claim at the end of paragraph 6 is limited to "federated training of Text-to-SQL models", which is narrow.
- The differences from FedDPO are listed (fixed pairs, a fixed reference model, no NLL term). The introduction does not say whether any of them is expected to matter, or why.

**3. Nothing in the introduction isolates execution feedback as the source of the gain. Major.**
- The only comparison named is FedAvg SFT. The introduction attributes the gain to "this signal" (abstract) and to the clients' databases as "a source of supervision" (contribution 1).
- A gain over SFT does not separate three things: the execution labels, the DPO objective, and the extra client compute.
- Simple controls are not mentioned: SFT on the correct self-samples (rejection sampling), longer local SFT, centralized CodeLutra as an upper bound, and local-only training. Section 6 is promised to show "what each part contributes", but the introduction does not say against what.

**4. The error counts suggest where the gain comes from, and the introduction does not connect them. Minor.**
- On BIRD, the 6.19-point gain is about 95 of the 1534 dev questions. Execution errors fall by 140 (268 → 128).
- On Spider, the gain is about 75 of 2147 questions, and errors fall by 89.
- In both cases the drop in errors is larger than the gain in correct answers. Most of the gain may therefore come from turning non-executing queries into executing ones.
- The introduction presents the error reduction as a separate benefit ("fewer of its predictions end in an execution error") rather than as a possible explanation for the gain.

**5. Running sampled queries on the organizations' own databases is presented as free. Minor.**
- Paragraph 2 motivates the setting with "a hospital or a bank" whose databases "are internal".
- The method then executes many model-generated queries per question on those databases. That raises load, runtime, and safety concerns (statements that write to or change the database, long-running queries).
- The introduction mentions none of this. The only cost it names is "computation at the clients", and the experiments use BIRD and Spider copies.

**6. The execution label is treated as ground truth. Minor.**
- Paragraph 4: "correct if its execution result equals that of the gold SQL query".
- Matching results can label a wrong query correct, for example when results coincide on small databases. Here those labels choose the preferred side of each pair. Label noise is not mentioned.

**7. Weighting by the number of pairs is unexplained. Minor.**
- Paragraph 7: "the number of pairs that weights it".
- A client on whose databases the model gets everything wrong, or everything right, forms few or no pairs, so its update gets little or no weight. That may down-weight exactly the hardest clients. The introduction gives no reason for this choice.

**8. Several citations do not support the sentences they are attached to. Minor.**
- "[16] now reaches models of 0.5B to 1.5B parameters": Section 2.1 gives [16] (OmniSQL) as 7B to 32B.
- "as the label of the pairs in direct preference optimization (DPO) [29]": [29] appears to be the DPO paper. On that reading, no cited work in this sentence shows execution labels used with DPO. CodeLutra [31] only appears in the next sentence.
- "Language models are commonly fine-tuned across clients by FedAvg with … LoRA [11]": [11] supports LoRA, not the claim that this federated practice is common.
- CodeLutra is called "the closest work to ours", but the introduction never says whether it addresses Text-to-SQL.

**9. Contribution 1 overclaims. Minor.**
- "We identify the databases of the clients as a source of supervision … the first method that uses it".
- Paragraph 4 has already shown that execution feedback is established at one site. What is new is its use in federated Text-to-SQL, which is narrower than "identify".

**10. Names and terms are not settled. Remark.**
- "FedLS" is never expanded.
- The baseline is called "federated SFT", "federated averaging with SFT" and "FedAvg SFT".
- The extra loss term is called "a likelihood term" (abstract) and "an NLL term" (Section 1).
- Wrong samples are "incorrect" in some places and "failed" (for CodeLutra) in others.

## 2. What I could not reproduce without guessing

- **Model:** which 1.5B-parameter model is used. Also why 1.5B: no reason is given beyond the trend in paragraph 1, and only one size is reported.
- **Client splits:** how databases are assigned to the five clients (randomly, balanced by questions, or otherwise). For Spider, whether `train_others` is included.
- **Training runs:** whether a separate model is trained for each benchmark. The phrase "BIRD … or Spider" suggests so.
- **BIRD evaluation:** whether BIRD dev is evaluated with or without the external-knowledge evidence.
- **Sampling and pairs:** how many samples per question and at what temperature. How pairs are formed (all correct × incorrect pairs, or one per question). What happens when all samples are correct or all are incorrect. Whether the gold query is ever used as the preferred sample.
- **Hyperparameters:** β, the weight of the NLL term, LoRA rank, local epochs, learning rate, and whether all five clients take part in every round.
- **Baseline schedule:** how many rounds FedAvg SFT was run. "Does not improve after round 3" may rest on round 4 alone.
- **Statistics:** McNemar p-values, the number of seeds, and variation across client splits.
- **Release:** no link for the code, splits and predictions promised in contribution 4.
