## 1. Findings

**F1. Novelty against CodeLutra is not stated (major).** Section 1, ¶4 and ¶6–7.
- ¶4 describes CodeLutra [31] as: sample from the current model, label by execution, pair a correct with a failed sample, train with DPO + NLL.
- ¶6–7 describe FedLS-SQL in almost the same terms ("samples several queries… labels them by execution… pairs correct with incorrect samples… DPO loss, and an NLL term on the correct sample").
- As I read it, the method is CodeLutra's loop run inside FedAvg, with SFT warm-up rounds first. The section never says what federated training made hard or what had to change. The only federated-specific choices visible are the reference model (the round's global model) and the aggregation weight (number of pairs), and neither is argued for.
- The FedDPO contrast in ¶5 positions the method against a weak foil, not against its closest neighbour.
- I expected one of two things: a baseline that applies the centralized method to pooled data, or an explanation of why that comparison is the wrong one. Neither appears.

**F2. The comparison protocol behind the headline number is unclear (major).** Results paragraph: "After 4 rounds… 48.76% … against 42.57%", "FedAvg SFT does not improve after round 3".
- I could not tell how the 4 rounds divide into warm-up and preference rounds.
- I could not tell how round 4 was chosen. BIRD dev is the evaluation set, and no validation set is mentioned.
- If FedAvg SFT is flat or falls after round 3, measuring it at round 4 rather than at its best round may inflate the gap.
- "At the same number of rounds" equalises communication, not compute: each preference round adds sampling, execution and reference-model passes. The reader cannot judge whether a compute-matched baseline would close the gap.

**F3. The federated premise is asserted and the clients are undefined (major).** ¶2: "The data needed to fine-tune such a model are distributed over organizations that cannot pool them… The aim is nevertheless a single model that serves all of the organizations".
- There is no citation and no concrete case for either the constraint or the wish for a shared model.
- Local fine-tuning is never offered as the alternative and ruled out.
- "With five clients" is the only description of the experimental federation. Nothing says the clients are cut from the public Spider/BIRD training sets, whether the split is by database, or how heterogeneous the clients are. The size of the gain cannot be interpreted without this.

**F4. Execution-based RL is missing from the introduction (minor, close to major).**
- ¶4 presents execution-label training as DPO ("models are already trained on such labels with DPO [29]").
- §2.1 then shows that SLM-SQL, Think2SQL and Arctic-Text2SQL-R1 train with execution-based RL rewards.
- In ¶1, SLM-SQL appears only as a size range, although it sits in the same 0.5–1.5B class and uses execution feedback.
- Why DPO rather than RL with an execution reward is never motivated.
- The absolute level (48.76% BIRD dev at 1.5B) is never placed against that line. If I recall correctly, SLM-SQL reports a BIRD dev EX in the 60s at 1.5B. The reader therefore wonders whether the base setup is weak.

**F5. A general claim is contradicted two paragraphs later (minor).** ¶3: "Federated fine-tuning of language models, as currently practiced, learns from the gold SQL query alone."
- As written, this covers all federated LM fine-tuning, yet ¶5 describes FedDPO, which is federated LM fine-tuning on preference pairs.
- A federated RLHF/preference line also exists more broadly.
- The claim only holds if restricted to Text-to-SQL.

**F6. "Both differences are significant" has no support (minor).** Results paragraph.
- No test is named.
- The number of runs or seeds is not given.

**F7. Client-side costs and risks are skipped (minor).** "Its additional cost is computation at the clients."
- The cost is not quantified: samples per question, execution time on BIRD-size databases, and the reference forward passes.
- Running model-generated SQL on an organization's internal databases raises load, timeouts, and the need for read-only access or sandboxing. The section treats this as free.
- The integer sent to the server (number of pairs) reveals how often the model is right and wrong on that client's data. This is a small leak, but the abstract and ¶7 present "only the adapter and one integer" as clean.

**F8. The role of the databases and of the gold query is described inconsistently (remark).**
- The abstract and ¶2 say fine-tuning "requires… the databases", but ¶3 says that under SFT the databases "serve only to construct the prompt".
- ¶4 says "Execution provides a label that the gold SQL query alone does not", yet the label is defined by comparison with the gold query's result. I had to reread this to see that the claim is "gold + execution", not "execution instead of gold".
- Execution-match labels can be false positives (a query that matches the gold result on this database content by coincidence). This is not acknowledged.

**F9. A citation does not support its claim (remark).** ¶4: "models are already trained on such labels with direct preference optimization (DPO) [29]".
- [29] is cited as the DPO method itself, not as work that trains Text-to-SQL models on execution labels.
- Apart from CodeLutra, the claim has no supporting citation.

**F10. Wording problems (remark).**
- ¶5: "keeps one reference model for all rounds and has no NLL term. Its pairs are therefore not outputs of the model being trained". "Therefore" does not follow from the reference model or the NLL term; it follows only from the earlier clause about the human-preference dataset.
- ¶6 says "many correct and incorrect queries" while ¶7 says "several queries per question".
- "By federated Text-to-SQL, we mean…" defines a term that the section never uses again.
