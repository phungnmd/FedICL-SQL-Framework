# Review of Section 3 (Method), FedExec-SQL manuscript

I read Section 3 from line 7 to line 131 and then Table 1, without stopping. I slowed down at the timeout cases in §3.5 (lines 71–79) and at the warm-up counts (lines 56–60). I left out the unwritten sections and appendices, and anything they point to.

## 1. Findings (most important first)

1. **Preference rounds drop the gold queries and the hardest questions** (§3.3 l. 42–43, §3.4 l. 56–60, §3.6 l. 94–96). *Major.*
   - The text says the framework trains on execution-derived supervision "in addition to the gold SQL queries". It also says the loss "retains an NLL term" (l. 10).
   - In a preference round, though, the gold query is used only to label samples. The NLL term acts on a sampled y⁺, never on y⋆.
   - A question whose samples are all incorrect contributes nothing. After warm-up that is 2,698 of 9,428 BIRD questions (28.6%), and they are the hardest ones. FedAvg SFT keeps training on them.
   - Weighting clients by pair count (l. 122–124) adds to this. A client with more hard questions (up to 58% in Table 1) gives fewer pairs and so gets less weight.
   - I expected the section to say this directly. It is mentioned only as a forward pointer ("the gold SQL query in place of a missing correct sample", l. 99–100).

2. **"Correct" means a matching execution result, and the section treats that as ground truth** (§3.2 l. 31: "A SQL query is verifiable by execution"). *Major.*
   - A semantically wrong query can return the same result as the gold query on one database instance. The section only deals with the extreme case of tables with no rows (l. 85–86).
   - These false positives then go in as y⁺, so both the DPO term and the NLL term reinforce them.
   - The section does not discuss this label noise or estimate how often it happens.

3. **"Renewed in every round" amounts to one renewal at the default settings** (l. 9–10, l. 51, l. 92: "Each federated round thereby becomes one resampling step"). *Minor.*
   - With T = 4 and T_w = 2 there are two preference rounds, so pairs and the reference model are rebuilt once after the first.
   - This is the main contrast with FedDPO, yet the "resampling" framing rests on a single step.

4. **The timeout handling departs from the evaluation it claims to follow** (§3.5 l. 71–79). *Minor.*
   - Labels are said to follow "the comparison of the evaluation metric". But a BIRD sample that runs out the shared time limit before the gold query finishes gets no label. In BIRD's own evaluation a timeout scores as incorrect.
   - This rule systematically leaves slow correct samples unlabeled.
   - Because labels depend on timing, two identical query strings could get different labels. Since identical samples are not merged (l. 89–90), a pair (y, y) with m_θ ≡ 0 could form.
   - "Exceeds the time limit on its own" (l. 79) does not fit one limit covering sample and gold together. When is the gold query timed separately?

5. **The base model is never named, yet a precise adapter size is given** (§3.1 l. 14–16, §3.7 l. 120: "its adapter (73.9 MB)"). *Minor.* Without the model, LoRA rank, target modules and storage precision, I cannot check the 73.9 MB.

6. **The warm-up statistics are not comparable** (§3.2 l. 37–40, §3.4 l. 55–60). *Minor.*
   - §3.2 gives the share of mixed-outcome questions (both a correct and an incorrect sample) after warm-up (62.3%).
   - §3.4 gives counts of all-incorrect questions before and after warm-up (4,035 → 2,698).
   - The quantity that matters for pairs is the mixed-outcome count under the pretrained model, and it is not given. So I cannot tell whether warm-up increases pair-yielding questions or mainly turns questions into all-correct ones.
   - "Both a correct and an incorrect sample" also differs from "yields pairs", which additionally requires tables with rows (l. 85).
   - All figures come from one sampling pass and BIRD only.

7. **Table 1 labels.** *Minor.*
   - "Hard or extra (%)" uses Spider's difficulty levels. BIRD's levels are simple, moderate and challenging, so the BIRD row's 25–58 has no stated definition.
   - The "IID" row has 29–30 databases per client, which is 146/5. That is a split by database, not IID over questions, and its wide hard-question range (34–52%) fits that reading.

8. **Client participation is inconsistent** (§3.1 l. 19: "all K clients participating in each" vs. §3.7 l. 124 and Alg. 1 l. 12: a client without pairs "sends no update").
   - In preference rounds, participation depends on the data.
   - If every client has P = ∅, Alg. 1 l. 18 (w_k ∝ |P_k^t|) is undefined. *Remark.*

9. **The privacy statement is stronger than the protocol** (l. 125: "Samples, execution results, execution labels and preference pairs remain at the client").
   - The uploaded |P_k^t| equals M times the number of mixed-outcome questions. That is a statistic of the execution labels, sent every round. *Remark.*

10. **Unexplained scaling and citation.** *Remark.*
    - Eq. (3) sums log-likelihoods over tokens with β = 0.1. The NLL term in Eq. (4) is a token mean with λ = 1. So the relative weight of the two terms changes with the length of the sequences, and no reason is given for this choice (l. 107–108).
    - Reference [3] is cited both for FedAvg SFT (l. 22, "[2,3]") and for FedDPO (l. 81). I could not tell what [3] is.

## 2. What I could not reproduce without guessing

- **Base model and adapter:** the base model, LoRA rank and target modules, and the precision behind 73.9 MB.
- **SQL extraction:** how SQL is pulled out of a generation. What counts as "completion" (EOS? a terminator?) versus "contains no query" (l. 75–77)?
- **Time limits:**
  - the values of the limits;
  - which Spider evaluator defines a per-query limit;
  - whether the BIRD gold query is executed once per question or once per sample (l. 72 suggests per sample);
  - how "on its own" (l. 79) is measured.
- **Empty databases:** "the tables of its database contain rows" (l. 85). All tables, any table, or the tables the gold query uses?
- **BIRD prompt:** whether it also contains sample rows. The sentence at l. 16–18 can be parsed either way.
- **Reference model:** whether the reference log-likelihoods and the policy forward pass both have LoRA dropout off. Otherwise m_θ = 0 at the start (l. 110) does not hold exactly.
- **The 62.3% figure:** the seed and sampling setup behind it. With temperature 1 a single pass varies.

## 3. What I did not believe, or found unsupported

- l. 31, "verifiable by execution": see finding 2.
- l. 28–29, "the model receives no signal about which of its own queries are incorrect": teacher-forced NLL does lower the probability of alternative tokens. What is missing is a sequence-level signal on the model's own samples. The sentence overstates this.
- l. 85–86, "without rows, execution results do not discriminate between queries": they still separate queries that fail from queries that run.
- l. 110–111, m_θ = 0 "for every pair at its start": this holds only if training-time stochasticity such as dropout is off.
- l. 63–64, the pretrained model "already yields preference pairs": no count is given, so this is true but says nothing about how many.
- l. 125, the privacy claim: see finding 9.
- l. 81–82, the description of FedDPO's data: this rests on [3] alone, and its citation is ambiguous (finding 10).

- **Participation:** l. 19 vs. l. 124 / Alg. 1 l. 12.
- **Labels vs. evaluation:** l. 73–74 (labels follow the evaluation metric) vs. l. 77–78 (BIRD timeouts left unlabeled).
- **Fig. 1:**
  - The arrow is labelled "Global model θ^{t−1}", but the text says the server sends the adapter.
  - The top box fixes "2 FedAvg rounds", while §3.4 treats T_w as a setting that can be 0.
  - The server box says "weighted by number of pairs" without saying this applies only to preference rounds.
- **Table 1:** "Hard or extra" applied to BIRD, and "IID" applied to a split by database.
- **Citations:** [3] is cited for FedAvg SFT and for FedDPO.
