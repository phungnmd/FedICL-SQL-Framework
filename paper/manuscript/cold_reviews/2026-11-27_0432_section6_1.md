## 1. Findings

**1. §6, "Without the preference term, more execution errors remain." (Table 8, lower block). Major.**
- The paragraph opens with execution-error counts (248 vs 102), which are not tested. The EX comparison it reports is not significant on either set: 44.39 vs 46.22 (p = 0.057) and 75.87 vs 77.46 (p = 0.015).
- Measured from the round-2 model (42.70 / 74.10), the NLL-only round gets +1.69 of the +3.52 on BIRD and +1.77 of the +3.36 on Spider. That is about half of the first-round gain with no incorrect sample in training. It is also above a third FedAvg SFT round (43.68 / 74.71, §5.1).
- I finished the paragraph not knowing what the preference term adds in EX. The section never says.
- The lower block has no "FedAvg SFT, 3 rounds" row, which is what you need to see this.
- This variant also trains only on questions that have an incorrect sample, so it is not plain NLL on all of the model's own correct samples.

**2. §6, "whereas test-suite accuracy is lower without them … The EX gain over FedAvg SFT thus carries over to TS with two warm-up rounds (Section 5.3) and does not without them." Major.**
- In Table 6, only one of the three TS differences between two and zero warm-up rounds survives Holm (Spider-Syn). Dev (p = 0.01) and Realistic (p = 0.0067) do not.
- With two warm-up rounds, the TS gain over FedAvg SFT is significant only on Spider-Syn (p = 0.027 and 0.036 elsewhere).
- "Does not without them" rests on a comparison that is never tested: no-warm-up against FedAvg SFT.
- Spider-DK goes the other way (+3.55 for no warm-up), and nothing explains it.
- So a conclusion in both directions rests on one significant set out of three.

**3. §6, "Without renewal of the pairs and the reference model …" (row "Fixed pairs and reference"). Major.**
- The pairs and the reference model change together, so the row cannot tell which of them matters. Yet §3.4 credits the pairs ("cannot follow the model as it changes").
- On Spider the test cannot show anything: the second preference round itself adds only +0.74 (p = 0.093, §5.1). "Level" therefore tells us nothing there.
- The evidence for renewal, which is central to the claimed contribution, comes down to one dataset and one seed.

**4. §6, "it does not raise EX: … 39.24 … with these pairs and 41.85 without them, not a significant difference (p = 0.0073)". Minor, but it points to a statistical problem.**
- This is a drop of 2.61 points (40 questions), and "does not raise" understates it.
- §4.6 says text-only comparisons form "a family of their own" but does not say what is in the family:
  - If the family is §6's own text, it holds this one comparison, and p = 0.0073 is significant.
  - If it is every text-only p-value in §§5–6, the verdict sits right at the Holm threshold.
- The experiment was run only with T_w = 0. It therefore says nothing about FedLS-SQL as proposed, where 2,698 BIRD questions still yield no pair.

**5. Ablations that are missing. Minor.**
- §2.2 lists three ways FedLS-SQL departs from CodeLutra: warm-up rounds, fewer pairs (M = 2 instead of 10), and an NLL averaged over tokens. §3.4 notes that the averaging changes the weight of the NLL term by a factor of |y⁺|.
- Only the warm-up rounds are ablated. M, N, β and λ are never varied.

**6. §6, "The weights of the server average do not change EX." Minor.**

**7. §5.4 tail, p. 16, the counts of correct predictions that read the gold tables and columns. Major for the paper.**
