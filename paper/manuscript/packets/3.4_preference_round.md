# Packet: Section 3.4, A preference round

Part of Section 3 (Method). Label of the subsection: `sec:pref-round`.

## What the subsection is for

It tells the reader exactly what a client and the server do in one
preference round, and why each step is the way it is. It is the core of
the method.

Before it the reader has: the setting and the notation of Section 3.1
(below), the overview with Fig. 1 (`fig:overview`, Section 3.2), and the
warm-up rounds (Section 3.3, `sec:warmup`): two rounds of FedAvg SFT on
the gold SQL, which leave the global model $\theta^{T_w}$.

After it the reader can reconstruct a round from the text alone, and
knows what leaves a client. Algorithm 1 (`alg:method`) lists the steps;
what a round costs is the next subsection (`sec:cost`); settings of the
optimizer are in Section 4 (`sec:setup`) and Appendix B.

## What it has to establish

| 3 | The loss has two terms. ## Facts

Notation (fixed in Section 3.1):

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $K$, $k$ | number of clients, a client |
| $\mathcal{D}_k$ | the questions of client $k$; an element is $(q, d, y^\star)$: question, database, gold SQL |
| $x$ | the prompt built from $q$ and the schema of $d$; one prompt for training, sampling and evaluation |
| $T$, $T_w$ | rounds in all, warm-up rounds; rounds $T_w + 1, \dots, T$ are preference rounds |
| $\theta^{t}$ | the global adapter after round $t$; $\pi_{\theta}$ the model with adapter $\theta$ |
| $N$, $M$ | samples per question, pairs per question |
| $\mathrm{exec}(y, d)$ | the result of query $y$ on database $d$ |
| $y^{+}$, $y^{-}$ | the correct and the wrong sample of a pair |
| $\mathcal{P}_k^{t}$ | the pairs of client $k$ in round $t$ |
| $\beta$, $\lambda$ | scale of the preference term, weight of the NLL term |

A preference round $t$, in order:

1. The server sends $\theta^{t-1}$ to every client. All clients take
   part in every round.
2. Sampling. For each $(q, d, y^\star) \in \mathcal{D}_k$ the client
   draws $N$ = `\setting{pref.samples}` (10) queries from
   $\pi_{\theta^{t-1}}(\cdot \mid x)$ at temperature `\setting{pref.t}`
   (1), with no nucleus or top-k cut, at most `\setting{pref.tokens}`
   (256) new tokens.
3. Labels. The client runs the gold SQL once and each sample on $d$.
   A sample $y$ is correct when $\mathrm{exec}(y, d) =
   \mathrm{exec}(y^\star, d)$, compared as the benchmark's execution
   accuracy compares results. Every other sample is wrong, and is one of
   two kinds: an execution error (it does not run, or exceeds the time
   limit) or a wrong result (it runs and returns another result). An
   output with no SQL in it is wrong; a sample cut off at the token
   limit gets no label and enters no pair.
4. Pairs. For a question with at least one correct and at least one
   wrong sample the client draws $M$ = `\setting{pref.pairs}` (2) pairs:
   $y^{+}$ at random from its correct samples, $y^{-}$ at random from
   its wrong samples. The draw is over the samples as the model produced
   them, so a query sampled three times is three times as likely to be
   drawn. No pair comes from: a question whose samples are all correct
   or all wrong; a question on a database whose tables hold no rows
   (there every query that runs returns the gold result, so the label
   says only whether a query runs); a question whose gold SQL does not
   run.
5. Local training. The client starts from $\theta^{t-1}$ and trains
   for `\setting{pref.epochs}` (1) epoch on $\mathcal{P}_k^{t}$ with the
   loss below, giving $\theta_k^{t}$.
6. The client sends $\theta_k^{t}$, the adapter
   (`\setting{adapter.mb}`, 73.9 MB), and nothing else.
7. The server averages every matrix of the adapter:
   $\theta^{t} = \sum_k w_k \theta_k^{t}$ with
   $w_k = |\mathcal{P}_k^{t}| / \sum_j |\mathcal{P}_j^{t}|$.
   (In a warm-up round the weights are the question counts $n_k$.)

The loss of one pair $(x, y^{+}, y^{-})$, as the code computes it:

$$ m_\theta = \beta \left[ \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y^{+} \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{+} \mid x)} - \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y^{-} \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^{-} \mid x)} \right] $$

$$ \ell(\theta) = -\log \sigma(m_\theta) \; - \; \frac{\lambda}{|y^{+}|} \log \pi_\theta(y^{+} \mid x) $$

- $\log \pi(y \mid x)$ is the sum of the log-probabilities of the tokens
  of $y$; $|y^{+}|$ is the number of tokens of $y^{+}$; $\sigma$ is the
  logistic function.
- $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}} = \pi_{\theta^{t-1}}$, the global model the round
  started from. It is fixed during the round: its log-probabilities of
  the pairs are computed once, before local training.
- $\beta$ = `\setting{pref.beta}` (0.1), $\lambda$ =
  `\setting{pref.nll}` (1).
- The first term is the DPO loss; the second is the mean negative
  log-likelihood of the correct sample. The client minimises the mean
  of $\ell$ over $\mathcal{P}_k^{t}$.

| `tao2024codelutra` | CodeLutra: for code and SQL generation on one machine, samples outputs from the model, labels them by execution against the gold result, pairs correct with wrong samples and trains with a DPO term plus a supervised term, repeating for several iterations.
