# Knowledge Distillation Review for FedLS-SQL

> **Status:** research and method-selection note, updated 2026-08-24.  The
> canonical implemented method remains defined by
> [`system_architecture.md`](system_architecture.md); empirical claims remain
> governed by [`LAB_LOG.md`](LAB_LOG.md) and
> [`../results/MAIN_RESULTS.md`](../results/MAIN_RESULTS.md).  A method discussed
> here is not a FedLS-SQL component until it is implemented, ablated, and promoted
> in those canonical records.

## 1. Scope and decision constraints

This note reviews recent KD methods and evaluates them for the actual FedLS-SQL
setting rather than for an unconstrained LLM-compression setup.  The relevant
constraints are:

1. **Federated privacy boundary.** Private Spider questions, schemas, databases,
   and SQL remain at clients.  The server teacher may access only a public proxy
   corpus and its public databases.  Only LoRA adapter parameters cross the
   client-server boundary.  This is structural data isolation, not differential
   privacy or secure aggregation.
2. **Small deployed model.** The student must remain below 3B parameters.  The
   primary student is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; the portability student is Gemma-2-2B-it.
3. **One workstation GPU.** The target server is one NVIDIA RTX A5000 with 24 GB
   GDDR6.  NVIDIA's official specification confirms the 24 GB capacity
   ([NVIDIA RTX A5000](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/rtx-a5000/)).
4. **Multi-round and multi-seed cost matters.** A method that fits 24 GB once can
   still be infeasible if it reloads the teacher, regenerates sequences, or
   rebuilds logits in every federated round and seed.
5. **Public proxy is necessary.** Server KD uses public BIRD examples and public
   BIRD databases.  A candidate that requires the teacher to see private client
   prompts is outside the current privacy contract.
6. **Text-to-SQL is verifiable.** Public SQL can be parsed and executed.  This
   gives a stronger correctness signal than an LLM judge and should be exploited
   before adding a reward model.

The decision criterion is therefore not just benchmark accuracy.  It is:

```text
expected transferable gain
-----------------------------------------------
VRAM × GPU-hours × implementation risk × scope
```

### Decision summary

There is no literature result that guarantees a method will beat the current
FedLS-SQL server stage.  The candidates answer different questions and should
not be ranked by reported headline gain alone:

| Question | Best candidate | Evidence and project fit | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| What should remain the paper's KD core? | Execution-verified hard SeqKD | Already gives the stable Qwen/Gemma gain; cacheable and tokenizer-independent | **Keep** |
| Which published method has the strongest task-specific prior? | KID | Designed for Text-to-SQL; reports up to `+5.83` average points, but needs changing imperfect prefixes and online teacher scoring | **Open P1.7 candidate; not yet scheduled** |
| What happened to the first project-specific bet? | Global-error execution-guided SeqKD | P0.9b loses 2.03 EX to a matched random subset and adds 18 execution errors | **Closed negative** |
| What happened to LLM-anchored FedDF? | Positive small screen, negative full-pool result | P0.10d gains 1.45 EX on 512 rows, but P0.10e loses 1.17 EX and adds 30 execution errors versus full hard-target CE | **Closed negative** |
| What is the cheapest loss-only probe? | Skew-RKL, then adaptive KL if needed | Reuses cached logits, but the expected ceiling is limited by the weak current RKL increment | **Archived option, not active** |
| What is the best offline extension under the innovation gate? | Execution-verified contrastive/preference KD | Converts teacher-correct versus student-failed SQL into reusable pairs; student-only training after pair construction | **Open P1.7 candidate; requires matched gate** |
| What should be deferred? | Full MiniLLM, GKD, SWITCH/SKD, cross-tokenizer logit KD | Requires on-policy/interactive teacher inference, invalidates the fixed cache, or expands the paper substantially | **Defer** |

Thus, **KID remains the strongest published Text-to-SQL-specific candidate**,
while P0.10e shows that LLM-anchored FedDF does not scale from its positive
512-row screen. The current method is the canonical fallback, not a permanent
research ceiling. A further KD/Federated proposal may enter P1.7 when it is
materially different from P0.9/P0.10 and defines its paper contribution,
matched control, fixed budget, staged promotion, and stop rule before training.

## 2. Notation and KD axes

For a public input `x` containing a question and schema, let:

- `y*` be the public gold SQL, used primarily as an execution oracle;
- `y_T` be SQL generated by the frozen teacher;
- `p_T(. | prefix, x)` be the teacher next-token distribution;
- `q_theta(. | prefix, x)` be the student distribution;
- `theta_FL,t` be the student LoRA adapter immediately after FedAvg at round `t`;
- `P_T` be the teacher-specific public pool retained after execution verification.

LLM KD methods differ along four independent axes:

| Axis | Main choices | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Training sequence | gold, teacher output, imperfect output, student output | Determines exposure bias and whether teacher logits are reusable |
| Transferred signal | hard sequence, logits, hidden states, rationale, preference | Determines information richness and memory cost |
| Divergence/objective | forward KL, reverse KL, skew KL, interpolated target, ranking | Determines mode coverage, stability, and capacity-gap behavior |
| Federated location | client, server, mutual, post-aggregation | Determines privacy, communication, and access to public data |

These axes must not be conflated.  For example, using reverse KL does not by
itself make a method MiniLLM, and placing KD after FedAvg does not by itself make
the method FedDF.

## 3. KD methods from the literature

### 3.1 Hard sequence or response distillation

The teacher first generates a target sequence and the student learns it with
ordinary causal cross-entropy:

```text
L_SeqKD = - sum_j log q_theta(y_T,j | y_T,<j, x)
```

This is often called sequence-level KD, response KD, or synthetic-data
distillation.  It requires only teacher outputs and supports different
tokenizers.  Its main weakness is that the student is trained on a fixed,
teacher-quality trajectory rather than on errors produced by its own policy.

Relevant results include:

- **Distilling Step-by-Step** adds teacher rationales as a second supervision
  target and reports that a T5-770M student can outperform few-shot PaLM-540B on
  one benchmark with less data
  ([Hsieh et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.507/)).
- **Orca** uses diverse explanation traces and progressive learning to train a
  13B student, demonstrating that data construction and explanation richness
  matter beyond simple imitation
  ([Mukherjee et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02707)).
- **DeepSeek-R1 distillation** fine-tunes Qwen/Llama students, including 1.5B,
  7B, 8B and 14B models, on filtered reasoning outputs from a much larger
  teacher.  It is strong evidence that high-quality verified response data can
  transfer reasoning to sub-15B students
  ([DeepSeek-AI, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)).
- A Text-to-SQL study comparing pure fine-tuning and pure KD reports that prompt
  and schema reduction materially improve efficiency, and that its distilled
  student improves test-suite accuracy by 1.9 points.  This is evidence that
  input construction can matter as much as the divergence choice
  ([Zhu et al., NAACL Industry 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-industry.5/)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** very high.  Teacher SQL is compact, directly verifiable,
cacheable, and does not reveal private data.  This is also the most strongly
supported component in the current experiments.

### 3.2 Forward-KL logit distillation

Classical KD minimizes:

```text
L_FKL = KL(p_T || q_theta)
```

Forward KL is conventionally described as mode-covering because it penalizes
the student for missing teacher probability mass.  However, recent LLM-KD
analysis finds that the usual mode-covering/mode-seeking account does not fully
predict optimization behavior: FKL emphasizes head tokens early, while RKL can
emphasize tail tokens early.  Therefore SQL structure alone is not sufficient
reason to prefer RKL without a matched experiment
([Wu et al., COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.383/)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** useful as a literature baseline, not the primary
candidate.  It uses the same cached logits as current RKL, so a tightly scoped
comparison is technically feasible if a reviewer requires it.

### 3.3 Reverse-KL distillation and MiniLLM

Reverse KL minimizes:

```text
L_RKL = KL(q_theta || p_T)
```

Reverse KL is conventionally described as mode-seeking because it discourages
the student from placing probability where the teacher assigns little mass.
That intuition is incomplete for finite LLM training, as noted above.
**MiniLLM** develops sequence-level
reverse-KL distillation for generative LMs and optimizes it using samples from
the student policy, with additional variance- and length-control mechanisms
([Gu et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543)).

The important distinction is:

- full MiniLLM uses student-generated trajectories and a sequence-level
  optimization derivation;
- FedLS-SQL currently computes token-level RKL under teacher forcing on a fixed
  verified teacher sequence.

Therefore FedLS-SQL is **MiniLLM-inspired in divergence direction**, but it does
not implement MiniLLM.

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** high as an auxiliary cached loss, but the current
cross-family evidence does not establish a stable incremental benefit over hard
teacher-target CE.

### 3.4 DistiLLM: skew-KL and adaptive off-policy KD

Plain RKL can be unstable when the student places mass in regions where the
teacher probability is nearly zero.  DistiLLM replaces the reference with a
teacher-student mixture and combines it with adaptive reuse of student outputs
([Ko et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03898)).  A simplified skew-RKL is:

```text
m_alpha = (1 - alpha) p_T + alpha q_theta
L_skewRKL = KL(q_theta || m_alpha)
```

The mixture bounds extreme disagreement and can stabilize the gradient.  The
full paper also contains an adaptive off-policy data strategy; merely changing
the divergence is not the full DistiLLM algorithm.

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** high for the loss-only variant.  It reuses the exact
teacher logit cache and adds no model.  The loss and centralized/client CLI
already support `rkl_skew_lambda`, but the federated runner does not yet expose
or fingerprint it.  A small configuration-plumbing change and tests are needed
before it is a valid FedLS-SQL arm.  This would implement only skew-RKL, not the
full DistiLLM sampling algorithm.

### 3.5 Adaptive divergence and adaptive teaching

Two newer lines directly challenge a fixed divergence over all tokens:

- **Adaptive KL (AKL)** combines forward and reverse KL according to training
  dynamics.  Its analysis reports that their head/tail emphasis changes over
  time and that adaptive combination outperforms either fixed direction
  ([Wu et al., COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.383/)).
- **Adaptive Teaching KD (ATKD)** assigns token-specific teaching modes and is
  motivated by the observation that a larger teacher can sometimes make a
  student worse.  It reports gains up to 3.04 average points across eight tasks
  ([Wu et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.587/)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** medium.  Both target the weaknesses suggested by the
current 7B-to-1.5B and 9B-to-2B setting: a capacity gap and heterogeneous token
difficulty.  A cached-logit approximation is possible, but neither is a
drop-in flag today.  AKL first requires a correct forward-KL path and schedule;
ATKD requires token-mode logic.  They rank after the skew-RKL sanity gate and
should be tested on a fixed T1 subset before full training.

### 3.6 GKD: on-policy distillation

GKD addresses training-inference mismatch by sampling outputs from the student
and asking the teacher to score those student trajectories:

```text
y_S ~ q_theta(. | x)
L_GKD = D(p_T(. | y_S,<j, x), q_theta(. | y_S,<j, x))
```

It can mix teacher-forced and student-generated sequences and can use different
divergences.  The method improves autoregressive distillation by letting the
student learn from its own mistakes
([Agarwal et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** methodologically strong but operationally expensive.
Every changing round adapter produces new student trajectories, invalidating
the fixed teacher-logit cache.  It can probably fit 24 GB if generation,
teacher scoring, and student training run sequentially, but full multi-round,
multi-seed GKD would consume substantially more GPU-hours than cached RKL.

### 3.7 KID: imperfect-data KD for Text-to-SQL

KID was proposed specifically for autoregressive Text-to-SQL.  It corrupts or
rewrites a target to create imperfect prefixes that approximate cascading
student errors, then performs KD on those prefixes.  The paper reports gains of
up to 5.83 average points over five Text-to-SQL benchmarks while targeting a
better performance-efficiency balance
([Zhong et al., EMNLP Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.403/)).

Conceptually:

```text
y_imperfect = Rewrite(student, y)
L_KID = CE(q_theta, y) + lambda KL(q_theta(. | y_imperfect), p_T(. | y_imperfect))
```

KID is cheaper than fully autoregressive on-policy sampling but still depends
on the current student.  Its prefixes change across steps/rounds and cannot use
the canonical fixed-target teacher cache.

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** relevant literature baseline, but not suitable as the
default multi-round server method on one A5000.  The training module contains a
KID path, but it is not wired as a federated arm and cannot consume the fixed
teacher cache.  A single-round or centralized matched probe therefore needs
runner integration plus live teacher scoring; it is possible, but not free.

### 3.8 TAID: temporally adaptive interpolation

TAID gradually shifts an intermediate target distribution from the student's
initial behavior toward the teacher.  It is designed to reduce teacher-student
capacity-gap problems, mode averaging, and mode collapse, and produced a
TAID-LLM-1.5B model
([Shing et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16937)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** potentially useful because the canonical gap is 7B to
1.5B.  A restricted fixed-trajectory interpolation may reuse cached teacher
logits, but it would be a TAID-inspired objective rather than the full published
training recipe.  It requires new schedule logic and a clean matched ablation,
so it ranks below the already implemented skew-RKL gate.

### 3.9 Speculative Knowledge Distillation

SKD lets the student propose tokens and lets the teacher replace low-ranked
proposals, producing trajectories that are both teacher-corrected and closer to
the student distribution.  It reports consistent gains across translation,
summarization, math, and instruction following
([Xu et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11325)).

SWITCH similarly lets the teacher intervene selectively during student
generation rather than scoring only a fixed teacher trajectory
([Wu et al., NAACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.206/)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** low under the current budget.  Interleaved generation
requires teacher participation during decoding, complicates KV-cache lifecycle,
cannot reuse the fixed cache, and increases both engineering and GPU-hour cost.

### 3.10 Preference and contrastive distillation

PLaD constructs pseudo-preference pairs in which a teacher response is preferred
to a student response, then optimizes a ranking objective instead of requiring
the student to match the full teacher distribution
([Zhang et al., ACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.923/)).

For Text-to-SQL, the pair can be verified rather than judged heuristically:

```text
chosen   = teacher SQL with EX = 1
rejected = current student SQL with EX = 0 or execution error
```

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** medium to high as an offline second stage.  Teacher SQL is
already available, student SQL can be generated once per selected round, and
preference training only loads the sub-3B student.  However, it adds a new
objective and compute-matching burden, so it is better treated as an extension
than as a late change to the canonical paper.

**DistiLLM-2** provides a closely related contrastive formulation: increase the
likelihood of teacher responses and decrease the likelihood of student
responses.  It improves over earlier KD methods across several generation tasks
([Ko et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ko25a.html)).  For
FedLS-SQL, execution makes pair construction unusually clean: retain a verified
teacher SQL as positive and a non-equivalent or non-executable student SQL as
negative.  This is promising, but it still needs a new trainer and careful
controls against merely adding more student updates.

### 3.11 Rationale and structured-CoT distillation

Rationale KD transfers intermediate reasoning rather than only final answers.
For Text-to-SQL, recent work reports that validated intermediate SQL steps help
especially on medium and hard queries
([Rationalization Models for Text-to-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06759)),
while Struct-SQL reports an 8.1-point improvement of structured over
unstructured CoT distillation
([Struct-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17053)).

The capacity caveat is important.  Students at or below 3B do not consistently
benefit from long reasoning traces; mixing short and long traces or matching
reasoning complexity to student capacity works better
([Li et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** medium only if the rationale is a short deterministic SQL
plan such as tables, joins, filters, aggregation, and nesting.  Verbose CoT is a
poor fit for a 1.5B student and changes output latency and method scope.

### 3.12 Cross-tokenizer and hidden-state KD

DSKD aligns teacher and student output spaces and supports KD across different
vocabularies
([Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1010/)).
It is useful for a Qwen-teacher-to-Gemma-student logit experiment.

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** low for the canonical study.  Qwen and Gemma currently use
same-family teacher/student pairs.  Cross-family hard sequence KD already works
without a shared tokenizer, while DSKD adds projectors, alignment logic, and new
failure modes.

### 3.13 Pretraining and pruning-oriented methods

- **MiniPLM** refines the pretraining data distribution with offline teacher
  knowledge and supports cross-family students
  ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea05e4fc0299c27648c9985266abad47-Abstract-Conference.html)).
- **Minitron** prunes depth/width/attention/MLP components and retrains the
  compressed model using KD
  ([Muralidharan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14679)).

**Fit for FedLS-SQL:** low.  The project post-trains an existing sub-3B student
with LoRA; it does not pretrain a model family or derive a new architecture by
pruning a large checkpoint.

## 4. Federated KD architectures

### 4.1 FedMD

FedMD uses a public dataset as a communication medium: heterogeneous clients
exchange predictions rather than requiring identical model parameters
([Li and Wang, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03581)).

FedLS-SQL differs because clients are homogeneous, exchange LoRA parameters, and
do not expose logits on public or private examples.

### 4.2 FedDF

FedDF first aggregates client models, then distills the ensemble of local models
into a server model on unlabeled proxy data
([Lin et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)).  Its server flow
is the closest architectural ancestor:

```text
client updates -> parameter average -> server public-data distillation
```

The teacher source is fundamentally different:

| Component | FedDF | FedLS-SQL |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher | Ensemble of client models | Frozen external server LLM |
| Distilled knowledge | Aggregated private-client behavior | Complementary public expert behavior |
| Public supervision | Unlabeled proxy inputs | Teacher-specific, execution-verified SQL and logits |
| Aggregation role | Distillation performs model fusion | FedAvg performs fusion; KD refines afterward |

FedLS-SQL should cite FedDF, but should not claim that server-side post-FedAvg
distillation alone is novel.

### 4.3 Public LLM assistance for private FL

Wang et al. study public data and public LLM distillation for private on-device
federated models, including public-data distribution matching and formal DP
settings
([NAACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.59/)).
This is important evidence that public LLM assistance can coexist with private
federated training.  FedLS-SQL differs in task, verification, LoRA exchange, and
the absence of a formal DP guarantee.

### 4.4 FedMKT

FedMKT performs selective mutual transfer between a server LLM and client SLMs,
including token alignment for heterogeneous vocabularies
([Fan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02224)).

FedLS-SQL is deliberately one-way:

- private client training updates the global SLM through LoRA FedAvg;
- the frozen LLM never sees private client prompts;
- LLM-to-SLM transfer happens only on the public proxy;
- the server LLM is not updated from client knowledge.

This simpler topology is cheaper and preserves the current privacy boundary,
but it should not be described as mutual knowledge transfer.

## 5. The current FedLS-SQL KD method

### 5.1 Public pool construction

For each frozen teacher lineage, FedLS-SQL constructs:

```text
P_T = {(x, y_T) in D_public : QuickExec_8s(y_T) = 1
                              and EX(y_T, y*) = 1}
```

The canonical Qwen teacher retains 3,873 examples.  The Gemma teacher is rebuilt
from all 9,428 BIRD training rows and retains 2,487 examples.  Counts, row
identities, targets, and logit caches are teacher-specific; the retained count
is not a transferable hyperparameter.

Gold SQL is used as an execution oracle and matched-control target, not as the
canonical training target.  This choice has two benefits:

1. hard targets are correct under the public executor;
2. teacher CE and teacher RKL are consistent because the target is a mode
   generated by that teacher.

It also has a limitation: conditioning only on teacher success can bias the pool
toward teacher-easy examples.  The current selector does not ask whether an
example is difficult or useful for the student.

### 5.2 Federated round

At round `t`:

```text
private client CE
  -> sample-weighted factor-wise FedAvg
  -> initialize theta_FL,t
  -> public teacher-target CE + cached token RKL
  -> theta_FedLS,t
```

Only LoRA adapters are uploaded and broadcast.  Server KD does not change the
network payload relative to pure FL.

### 5.3 Current objective

```text
L_server = lambda_CE CE(q_theta, y_T)
         + lambda_KD KL(q_theta || p_T)
```

The RKL is calculated only over the target span.  Teacher logits are stored in
fp16 on disk and loaded by exact content hash; the 7B teacher is not loaded
during cache-backed Qwen server training.  The current implementation also
slices away the schema-heavy prompt before the full-vocabulary projection used
by KD.

The correct method description is:

> FedLS-SQL performs execution-verified teacher-target sequence distillation
> plus token-level reverse-KL regularization in a server-refinement stage
> initialized from the FedAvg LoRA adapter.

It is not full MiniLLM, GKD, FedDF, or FedMKT.

### 5.4 What the current evidence establishes

The strongest repeatable mechanism is hard teacher-target transfer:

- Qwen T1, seed 0: public-gold CE is neutral versus FL (`+0.48 EX`, `p=0.800`),
  teacher-target CE beats matched public-gold CE by `+3.48 EX` (`p=0.0026`),
  and the full method beats public-gold CE by `+5.51 EX` (`p<1e-6`).
- Across three Qwen T1 seeds, the whole server stage adds `+4.55 +/- 1.75 EX`
  over FedAvg (`p=0.046`).
- The incremental Qwen RKL contrast is `+1.71 +/- 1.38 EX`, `p=0.165`, so it is
  provisional.
- Gemma T1: FL reaches `57.16`, teacher-target CE reaches `61.22`, and CE+RKL
  reaches `61.41`.  The RKL increment is only `+0.19`, `p=0.916`.

Therefore:

1. **supported core:** execution-verified teacher-generated hard targets;
2. **supported endpoint:** the whole server stage improves pure FL;
3. **not independently supported:** family-stable gain from plain RKL;
4. **novelty location:** the privacy-compatible federated LLM-to-SLM framework
   and verified public transfer, not a new divergence.

## 6. A5000 feasibility analysis

### 6.1 Memory profile

A 1.5B model has about 3 GB of fp16 weights before activations and framework
overheads; a 2B model has about 4 GB.  LoRA avoids optimizer states for all base
weights.  A 7B or 9B teacher should therefore be:

- run in 4-bit for offline generation/cache construction; or
- removed entirely from repeated server training by using the cache.

The dangerous object is not only model weights.  Token KD computes a
full-vocabulary distribution of roughly 150k tokens.  The project already
reduces this cost by using batch size 1, projecting only the target span, and
accumulating gradients.  A method that adds long reasoning targets or multiple
simultaneous candidate sequences can increase activation and logits memory even
when model parameters remain unchanged.

### 6.2 VRAM fit is not enough

The following methods can likely be made to fit 24 GB with sequential phases,
but have very different total cost:

| Method | Teacher in repeated training | Fixed cache reusable | Generation per round | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Teacher-target CE | no | target text | no | Main/core |
| Cached RKL | no | yes | no | Main auxiliary loss |
| Common-mask cross-family hard SeqKD | no | target text | no | Cheapest diagnostic |
| Student-aware selection/weighting | no | yes | student once | Best new-method fit |
| Cached skew-RKL / AKL | no | yes | no | Cheap loss gate |
| Fixed-trajectory TAID-inspired loss | no | likely | no | Secondary gate |
| KID | yes or rescore required | no | rewrite per step | Centralized/T1 probe only |
| GKD | rescore required | no | autoregressive | Future or small subset |
| SKD | interactive | no | interleaved | Defer |
| Offline preference KD | no after pair creation | pair cache | once per chosen round | Feasible extension |
| Short structured plan KD | no after target creation | target cache | offline teacher generation | Feasible but changes scope |

The cache-backed family preserves the ability to run matched controls and seeds.
On-policy methods spend the same scarce GPU budget on regenerating supervision,
which can make statistical replication less feasible even if a single run fits.

### 6.3 Sub-3B learnability

For a 1.5B/2B student:

- prefer compact SQL and short structured plans over verbose CoT;
- avoid forcing the student to cover the teacher's entire output distribution;
- mix easy and hard rows or use a curriculum rather than training only on the
  longest/hardest traces;
- preserve direct-SQL examples so inference does not require long reasoning;
- evaluate execution errors and `EX=1, EM=0`, not only average EX.

## 7. Feasible directions for this project

### Tier A0 — retain execution-verified teacher-target CE

This is the strongest established and most hardware-efficient mechanism.  It
should remain the core method unless a replacement wins a matched causal ladder
in both Qwen and Gemma.

### Tier A1 — common-mask cross-family hard SeqKD diagnostic

Train Qwen-1.5B and Gemma-2B on the same 2,019 public rows for which both
teachers produce execution-equivalent SQL.  Run both same-family and
cross-family hard-target assignments with identical initialization, update
count, and evaluation:

```text
Qwen student <- Qwen target / Gemma target
Gemma student <- Gemma target / Qwen target
```

Hard targets do not require aligned tokenizers, so this uses existing artifacts
and no new teacher inference.  It answers whether the gain comes mainly from
verified SQL supervision or from same-family teacher behavior.  It is a
**diagnostic/control**, not a proposed replacement objective.  If cross-family
targets remain competitive, the paper can make a stronger teacher-agnostic
framework claim and avoid cross-tokenizer logit KD.

### Tier A2 — federated-aware subset selection

**Post-P0.9 status: closed negative.** The implemented balanced hard subset is
worse than its random control on EX, EM, and execution validity. The diagnostic
signal predicts failure but does not produce a useful training curriculum.
Do not tune its ratio, add client disagreement, or rerun it with another loss.

Select a fixed budget of public examples using:

- student execution failure;
- student negative log-likelihood on `y_T`;
- student-teacher KL from the existing cache;
- SQL complexity strata such as joins, nesting, aggregation, and set operators.

The stronger variant also runs the already-uploaded client adapters on the same
public rows and measures agreement between their execution-result groups. This
does not add communication or expose private prompts: all inference is performed
at the server on public data. Use disagreement only if it predicts aggregate
errors beyond the global adapter's execution state; otherwise it adds cost
without making the selector more informative.

Train on the same number of examples and updates as a random-subset control.
This tests whether targeted proxy use improves sample efficiency before changing
the loss.  It is the cheapest direct test of the project's main new idea because
only the small aggregated student must generate predictions.

### Tier A3 — execution-weighted cached KD

**Post-P0.9 status: not active.** This uses the same global execution-state
signal as the failed selector. Smooth capped weights differ from hard
subsampling, but that distinction alone is insufficient to justify another run.
Reconsider it only if analysis identifies a specific coverage failure that a
prespecified weighting rule resolves.

At the start of server refinement, the aggregated 1.5B student generates one SQL
per public example.  The public executor assigns a difficulty state:

```text
e_i,t = 0  if student SQL has EX = 1
e_i,t = 1  if SQL executes but EX = 0
e_i,t = 2  if SQL has an execution error
```

Use this state only to weight or sample the existing verified teacher examples:

```text
w_i,t = normalize(1 + beta_1 I[e_i,t = 1] + beta_2 I[e_i,t = 2])

L_server,t = sum_i w_i,t [CE(q_theta, y_T,i)
                          + lambda_KD KL(q_theta || p_T,i)]
```

This is not full GKD: the loss still uses the verified teacher trajectory and
therefore reuses the canonical teacher cache.  It borrows GKD's student-aware
principle while exploiting SQL execution for hard-example mining.

**Why it is attractive:**

- teacher still sees only public data;
- no private prompt or client logit leaves a client;
- only the small student performs extra generation;
- no teacher rescore is needed;
- the public executor replaces a reward model;
- it addresses the current pool's teacher-success bias by asking which retained
  rows remain difficult for the student;
- execution-error rows can be emphasized separately from semantically wrong but
  valid SQL.

**Cost control:** generate once per selected round, store predictions and
execution statuses, cap weights, and begin with a subset/T1 gate.  Do not
regenerate per optimizer step.

**Mandatory ablations:** uniform sampling, confidence-only sampling, execution
weighting, and equal-number-of-updates control.  Otherwise a gain could be due
only to more repeats of some rows.

### Tier A4 — cached skew-RKL, then adaptive KL

**Research question:** is the weak portability of plain RKL caused by extreme
teacher-student probability gaps or by using one fixed divergence throughout
training?

First replace the RKL denominator with a teacher-student mixture while keeping
the same target, cache, steps, and initialization:

```text
teacher-target CE
CE + cached RKL
CE + cached skew-RKL
```

No new teacher inference is required, but the federated runner must first expose
and fingerprint `rkl_skew_lambda`.  Promote only if skew-RKL improves EX or
execution-error stability under matched updates.  If it fails, do not launch a
large divergence grid.  Implement an AKL-style forward/reverse schedule only if
token-level analysis shows complementary head/tail errors.  A loss-only change
is an ablation, not the paper's standalone contribution.

### Tier B1 — offline execution-verified preference distillation

Generate one student SQL per public row from `theta_FL,t`, pair it with verified
`y_T`, and retain pairs with a meaningful quality gap:

```text
chosen   = y_T, EX = 1
rejected = y_S, EX = 0 or execution error
```

Train the sub-3B student with a PLaD/DPO-style ranking objective after, or in a
small mixture with, teacher-target CE.

**Hardware:** pair construction and student training fit the A5000 because the
teacher target already exists and the repeated stage loads only the student.

**Scientific risk:** it introduces a second optimization family and may make the
paper harder to attribute.  Use it only after the current CE/RKL mechanism and
multi-seed endpoint are closed.

### Tier B2 — compact structured-plan distillation

Ask the teacher to emit a deterministic compact plan:

```text
tables | joins | predicates | aggregation | nesting | SQL
```

Validate the final SQL, cache the plan, and train with either a multi-task plan
head/format or a plan-then-SQL target.  Keep the plan short and include direct
SQL examples because the student is below 3B.

**Hardware:** feasible offline with teacher 4-bit and student LoRA; increased
target length raises activation/logit cost but should remain manageable with
batch 1 if plans are compact.

**Scope risk:** changes output format, decoding latency, and the no-reasoning
canonical contract.  It is better suited to a follow-up study unless complex-query
errors clearly dominate the current audit.

### Tier B3 — restricted TAID-inspired cached schedule

Interpolate the cached teacher distribution with a detached student reference
and gradually change the interpolation coefficient during server training.
This targets the 7B-to-1.5B capacity gap without generating new trajectories.

**Caveat:** this must be described as TAID-inspired fixed-trajectory KD, not full
TAID.  It ranks below skew-RKL because it requires new schedule choices and more
hyperparameter evidence.

### Tier C — defer under one A5000

| Direction | Why defer |
|---|---|
| Full GKD/Exec-GKD every round | New student generations and teacher scores invalidate cache; high multi-round GPU-hour cost |
| Full MiniLLM | Policy-gradient implementation and variance control are unnecessary for the current evidence gap |
| SKD | Interactive teacher-student decoding is expensive and complex |
| DSKD cross-tokenizer | Current same-family replication avoids the problem; engineering cost is high |
| Full mutual FedMKT | Would require a new privacy and communication protocol and possibly expose client-derived signals to the LLM |
| Long CoT distillation | Poor capacity match for 1.5B/2B and increases training/inference length |
| Minitron/MiniPLM | Wrong training stage: architecture compression/pretraining rather than federated post-training |
| Reward-model/RL pipeline | Adds another model or unstable optimization when a deterministic SQL executor already exists |

## 8. Recommended experiment sequence

This section specifies decision gates, not executable commands.

### Gate 0 — close current evidence

1. Freeze the current FL, hard-SeqKD, and CE+RKL checkpoints and paired outputs
   so a new method cannot silently replace the canonical baseline.
2. Finish the `EX=1, EM=0` and execution-error audit needed to define the
   student-aware strata.
3. Keep fixed-warm-up resource measurements and final seed replication in the
   submission queue, but do not require them before the cheap method probes.

Do not silently sacrifice submission-critical controls to run a large new KD
grid. A focused proposal may be reprioritized when its expected contribution
and compute cost are documented; the gates below are deliberately narrow and
stop early on a negative result.

### Gate 1 — zero-new-teacher diagnostic

Run the common-2,019-row same-family/cross-family hard SeqKD comparison.  This
is the cheapest way to determine whether teacher-family alignment matters and
whether the framework can be presented as teacher-agnostic.  Do not add
cross-tokenizer logits unless hard-target portability fails and that question
is important to the paper.

### Gate 2 — federated-aware selection probe (closed negative)

**Final result:** closed negative. `global_error256` scores `56.67` EX versus
`58.70` for `random256`, with 47/68 paired gains/losses (`p=0.0617`) and 240
versus 222 execution errors. It fails both promotion conditions. Steps below
are retained as executed design provenance.

**P0.9a decision:** steps 1--4 are complete on 512 public rows. High client
disagreement is associated with aggregate error, but it marks 454/512 rows and
does not significantly enrich uniform-SeqKD corrections within aggregate
errors (Fisher `p=0.297`). Therefore step 5 proceeds with global execution state
only; the disagreement arm is cancelled.

Using the frozen pre-server FedAvg adapter:

1. generate student SQL on a public subset;
2. execute and record correct/semantic-error/execution-error strata;
3. measure how these strata correlate with student-teacher KL, SQL complexity,
   and eventual per-example correction;
4. measure whether client execution-result disagreement adds predictive value
   beyond the aggregated student's status;
5. compare the activated 256-row global-error mixture against a teacher-token-
   matched 256-row random subset; do not add disagreement selection.

This probe requires no new teacher inference if the row is already in `P_T`.

### Gate 3 — execution-weighted cached KD

Cancelled as the automatic continuation of Gate 2. A future weighting proposal
must first explain why it avoids the observed loss of coverage and must enter as
a new preregistered hypothesis, not as P0.9 tuning.

### Gate 4 — cheapest objective test

On the primary Qwen T1 setting, compare target CE, cached RKL, and cached
skew-RKL with identical pool, steps, seed, initialization, and evaluation.  Stop
if skew-RKL gives no meaningful accuracy or error-stability gain.  Consider AKL
only if token-level diagnostics justify its added schedule.

### Gate 5 — portability

If the Qwen gate succeeds, rebuild the student status weights independently for
Gemma's own `P_T`.  Never reuse Qwen-selected row identities or Qwen difficulty
labels as the Gemma method, because that would condition the second-family result
on the Qwen lineage.

### Gate 6 — optional KID or contrastive extension

Choose at most one based on error analysis:

- errors cascade after imperfect prefixes -> a fixed-budget KID probe;
- many valid-but-wrong or non-executable student queries -> execution-verified
  contrastive/preference distillation;
- many complex-structure failures -> compact structured-plan distillation.

Do not run both without expanding the research scope and ablation budget.

## 9. Recommended paper positioning

The current contribution should be presented as a framework contribution:

> FedLS-SQL combines private client-side LoRA adaptation, sample-weighted
> federated aggregation, and execution-verified public LLM-to-SLM transfer.  A
> frozen server LLM generates teacher-specific public SQL targets that are
> filtered by execution equivalence; the aggregated sub-3B global model is then
> refined using teacher-target CE and an auxiliary token-level reverse-KL loss.

The paper should explicitly distinguish itself from related methods:

- unlike FedDF, the distillation teacher is an external frozen LLM rather than
  an ensemble of client models;
- unlike FedMKT, transfer is one-way and the LLM never receives private
  client-derived prompts;
- unlike MiniLLM/GKD, canonical training uses fixed verified teacher trajectories
  and an offline cache rather than student-generated on-policy trajectories;
- unlike generic response KD, SQL correctness is checked with the public
  executor before a target enters the teacher-specific pool.

Claims to avoid without new evidence:

- “FedLS-SQL implements MiniLLM/GKD/DistiLLM”;
- “reverse KL is the family-independent source of the gain”;
- “server-side post-FedAvg distillation is itself novel”;
- “raw-data isolation provides formal privacy”;
- “a 3,873-row public budget transfers across teachers”;
- “the LLM learns from private client knowledge.”

## 10. Final recommendation

### Post-P0.10e evidence update

Client-ensemble distillation passed a bounded screen but failed the unchanged
full-pool promotion gate on EX and execution validity. It is archived as an
internal negative experiment and is not part of the proposed method. Detailed
metrics, commands, and recovery references live in the closed-branch archive.

For the current paper and one RTX A5000 24 GB:

1. keep **execution-verified teacher-target CE** as the established core;
2. keep cached RKL as an auxiliary component but describe its independent
   evidence as provisional;
3. treat P0.9 global-error selection, client-disagreement selection, and their
   direct weighted/KL continuations as closed;
4. keep client-ensemble distillation closed in the internal archive;
5. discuss **cached skew-RKL** only as a cheap objective ablation, not a strong
   FL--KD integration claim;
6. retain **KID** and execution-verified preference KD as second- and third-line
   fallbacks supported only by feasibility diagnostics;
7. require full-scale confirmation before promoting any future small-budget
   method screen;
8. defer full GKD, MiniLLM, SKD, cross-tokenizer DSKD, mutual FedMKT, and long-CoT
   training under the present GPU-hour and scope budget.

The key design principle is:

> Use the large teacher once to construct verified, reusable public knowledge;
> do not assume that examples where the federated student fails are therefore
> the examples on which extra distillation will generalize best.
