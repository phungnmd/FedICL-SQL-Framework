# FedLS-SQL: direction scan of 2026-11-22

Owner's request (2026-11-22): look for a method direction that takes the
paper to a Q2 journal, from the task and the literature and not from what
the project already has; no niche conditions (no clients without gold SQL).
Added during the session: a direction that is too niche or has low odds is
dropped, not listed as an option.

Nothing was run on a GPU and nothing is decided. Section 4 is the session's
recommendation; the choice is the owner's.

The agents worked from the project's numbers of the morning, where no line
was separated from FedAvg SFT on BIRD dev. During the scan
`bird_k2/round_1` was read: 46.22, 2.54 above FedAvg SFT at 3 rounds
(p 0.0087; `LAB_LOG.md`). Sections 3 and 4 take that result into account;
the agents' odds do not.

## 2. What the scan settled

1. The gap is real. The only federated Text-to-SQL paper found is Zhang et
   al., ACL 2023 (arXiv 2305.17221: T5-base, exact match, eight datasets
   as clients). No paper of 2024 to 2026 trains a language model for
   Text-to-SQL in a federation, and none uses Spider or BIRD as federated
   data (S1, P0, both devil's advocates). Eight federated preference or
   GRPO papers appeared in 2026 (for example PubSwap 2604.12160, Fed-GRPO
   2610.11502, FedMOA 2602.00453); their tasks are chat, summarisation,
   mathematics, medicine and HumanEval. None trains on SQL or checks
   against a database. Limit: the list of papers citing Zhang et al. could
   not be queried.
2. Aggregation has nothing to repair. With LoRA on a decoder model the
   published gap between FedAvg and centralized training is small
   (FS-LLM Table 2: 13.29 against 13.54; FedDPA Table 1: 77.50 against
   78.60), as here (74.71 against 75.03 on Spider test). In Fed-GRPO the
   aggregation weight alone is worth 0.91 (its Table 4).
3. Learning from the execution of the model's own SQL at the clients is
   the only lever any report could support above centralized SFT. No
   second one survived the checks. Its parts are already separated on
   Spider test (one round from FedAvg SFT round 2; FedAvg SFT at the same
   count 74.71): NLL on the model's own correct samples 75.87, the
   preference term alone 73.59, both 77.46.
4. Why the papers disagree about DPO on execution labels. On top of a
   converged SFT model of 6.7B to 15B, plain SQL-only DPO moves ten
   models by -1.3 to +0.8 on BIRD dev, six of them down (Liu et al.
   2502.11656, Table 1); with an added SFT loss the same DPO gives +1.2
   (Table 7). The project's row without the NLL term agrees: 73.59
   against its start 74.10 on Spider test. From the base model with the NLL term it is large
   (CodeLutra). The 1.5B student here gains more from an SFT start than
   those 7B models: +2.54 on BIRD dev at 3 rounds, +3.5 on Spider test at
   4 rounds (k = 2).
5. BIRD has noisy gold and resolves small gains badly (A4, from six public
   audits). BIRD train: gold SQL corrected by experts in 52.1% of 2.5k
   random rows (ReViSQL 2603.20004); about half of those corrections are
   habits (ties, duplicates), and gold that is wrong in content is about a
   quarter of the set by two independent sources (24.6% and 26.8%). BIRD
   dev: 34% to 53% of sampled rows have an error (SALUS 2610.05540; Jin et
   al. 2601.08778). SALUS states in its abstract that two systems within
   5.09 points on BIRD dev cannot be told apart at 95%. Removing the rows
   judged wrong from training is worth -0.46 and -0.1 on the released dev
   (BIRD team's filtered set at 3B; ROUTE at 8B). Spider gold that is
   wrong in content is 2% to 6%.
6. The bar of Q2 journals (S5, 20 journal articles of 2024 to 2026). The
   usual Q2 method paper adapts a known mechanism with a one-sentence
   story, uses 1 or 2 benchmarks and 1 to 10 open models, compares with
   SFT and 1 to 5 published methods, gains +0.75 to +7 points, and shows a
   module ablation; 7 of 10 report one run without a test. Q1 papers
   differ in breadth, not in the size of the gain. S5 adds Electronics,
   Entropy, Applied Sciences and Mathematics as Q2 venues with recent
   papers on these topics; for CMC and JIIS, `JOURNAL_BAR.md` is the
   fuller read.

## 4. Recommendation

The scan did not find a mechanism that beats the current core. Ten
directions were proposed without regard to the project; under a
prior-work and cost check each one died or shrank to an extra part with
low odds. On the same day the core gave its first separated gain on BIRD.

1. Keep the core as the method. The scan gives it a checked gap (point 1)
   and its closest works: CodeLutra centrally, OpenFedLLM's fixed-pair
   FedDPO, the 2026 federated GRPO papers.
2. No second mechanism now. With gains of +3.5 on Spider test and +2.54
   on BIRD dev the method is inside what Q2 journals accept (point 6).
3. The compute goes to breadth, which is what separates accepted papers
   (section 6): the second and third model (queued 2026-11-22), the
   published federated baselines, the local-only row and the loop run
   centrally.
4. Two cheap reads for the paper's analysis, no training (section 5):
   voting and best of 8, and wrong gold among the questions no sample
   solves, with a verified column on BIRD.
5. Stop: new pair rules and loss variants inside a round, aggregation
   work, schema perturbation, student-written reasoning, steering of
   public data.

## 5. Probes

Hours are the agents' estimates from the measured costs of the project.

1. Voting and the best of 8 (about 1 GPU hour, no training). On existing
   checkpoints of FedAvg SFT and of the preference line, 8 samples at
   T 1.0 and the greedy output on Spider test and BIRD dev: greedy, vote
   over execution results, best of 8. It says whether voting closes the
   lead of the preference line, which a reviewer who knows voting will
   ask, and how much a selector could add. No selector if best of 8 minus
   vote is under 6 EX on either set.
2. Wrong gold among the unsolved questions (2 to 3 CPU hours, no
   training). Join the stored samples of BIRD train with BIRD-Platinum
   (2,462 audited rows keyed by the row index of `train.json`; GitHub
   `uiuc-kang-lab/ReViSQL`, no licence file) and with the BIRD team's
   filtered set (Hugging Face `birdsql/bird23-train-filtered`, CC BY-SA
   4.0). Count the share of content-wrong gold among the questions no
   sample solves against the questions some sample solves, by hardness
   and by client. It gives a table nobody has printed and says whether
   "gold as the chosen side hurts" comes from wrong gold. A4's counting
   steps and stop rules are in its section 5.
3. Only if the owner wants the hint: one preference round on BIRD with
   hint-sampled positives for the unsolved questions and a control arm
   with gold as the chosen side on the same questions (about 8 GPU hours
   an arm). The thresholds DA2 set were against 44.26 and have to be set
   again against the k = 2 line. It needs the owner's yes to a second
   sampling prompt.

## 6. Rows the paper needs whatever is chosen

- Local training only (mean over the clients). In Zhang et al. the best
  federated row only equals it; without this row the paper does not show
  that federating pays.
- The preference loop run centrally on the union of the clients.
- FedProx, a FedOPT-style row, Lorar (the method of the one earlier
  federated Text-to-SQL paper) and federated DPO as published.
- A second and a third model.
- If any row samples at test time: the same N for every row and a cost
  column.

## 7. Corrections and limits

- The brief given to the agents lacked the row "NLL on the model's own
  correct samples". DA1's remark that this part was never isolated does
  not hold for Spider (point 3); it holds for BIRD.
- SPFT-SQL (2509.03937) is not usable as evidence: its synthesis stage
  also changes the prompt (schema filtering, value matching), no prompt
  or code is published, and its untrained rows are 10 to 12 points above
  other papers'. This agrees with the owner's decision of 2026-11-20.
- SQL-Zero (2609.04697) shows that tasks made on training databases
  transfer to unseen ones at 3B; it does not show that they add to gold,
  and one iteration costs 18 to 24 H100 hours.
- Readings corrected by the devil's advocates: the 2.5% of the test-suite
  paper is exact set match, not EX (single-database EX false positives
  are 6.5% on average); Yang et al. repair 26% to 33% of the questions
  with no correct query in the top 10, not over 50%; the OmniSQL row 86.6
  / 59.6 is the row with reasoning, the plain row is 80.7 / 55.1; the
  SQLoom preprint is in a submission template, not accepted.
- Every claim that something does not exist rests on 3 to 27 searches by
  the agent that made it. Quartiles in S5 come from search summaries, and
  S5 left out 17 Elsevier articles it could not open.
- The probabilities are judgements of single agents, not measurements.
